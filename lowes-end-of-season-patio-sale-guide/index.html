How to maximize savings during end-of-season patio sales

By Groundwork Editorial • 5 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Learn why late summer is the best time to buy patio furniture and how to identify high-quality deals during end-of-season sales at major retailers.

In This Guide

  • Why is late summer the optimal time to buy patio furniture?
  • What should you look for when shopping clearance patio sales?
  • How do you assess the value of hardscape structures like gazebos?
  • How should you store clearance patio furniture for the winter?
  • Is it worth buying open-box or floor model furniture?

An end-of-season patio sale is a strategic retail event occurring in late summer, typically between August and September, where home improvement retailers reduce inventory to clear space for autumn and holiday merchandise. These sales offer significant price reductions on outdoor furniture, hardscape structures, and garden decor, allowing consumers to acquire durable goods at a fraction of their original retail cost.

Data indicates that outdoor furniture prices often drop by 30% to 50% during the late summer clearance cycle as retailers prioritize clearing floor space over maintaining high profit margins on seasonal inventory (Source: Consumer Reports). By understanding the timing and quality indicators of these sales, you can secure high-quality outdoor furnishings that will remain functional for years to come.

Why is late summer the optimal time to buy patio furniture?

Late summer is the optimal time to purchase patio furniture because retailers face physical space constraints that necessitate rapid inventory turnover. As demand for outdoor living products declines following the peak spring and early summer seasons, stores begin marking down inventory to make room for seasonal items like leaf blowers, snow removal equipment, and holiday decor. Purchasing during this window allows you to capitalize on the inverse relationship between seasonal demand and price.

Retailers such as Lowe’s utilize these clearance events to liquidate remaining stock, which often includes high-demand items like conversation sets, dining tables, and structural elements like pergolas. Buying now does not only save money; it also ensures your outdoor space is fully furnished and ready for the first signs of spring next year, avoiding the price premiums common during the high-demand spring season.

What should you look for when shopping clearance patio sales?

When shopping for clearance patio furniture, prioritize material durability and construction quality over aesthetic trends. Outdoor furniture is subjected to significant environmental stress, including UV radiation, moisture, and temperature fluctuations. Examine the frame materials, such as powder-coated aluminum, stainless steel, or high-density polyethylene (HDPE) lumber, as these materials offer superior longevity compared to untreated wood or thin-gauge steel (Source: Architectural Digest).

Follow these steps to evaluate potential purchases:

1. Inspect structural integrity: Ensure frames are rigid and hardware is made of stainless steel to prevent rust. 2. Verify fabric quality: Look for solution-dyed acrylic fabrics like Sunbrella, which resist fading and mildew better than standard polyester. 3. Check for modularity: If you are buying a set, confirm that individual pieces are available for replacement or expansion in the future. 4. Measure your space: Clearance items are often final sale; ensure the dimensions of the furniture accommodate your patio layout before purchasing.

How do you assess the value of hardscape structures like gazebos?

Hardscape structures, such as gazebos, pergolas, and solariums, represent the most significant potential savings in a clearance event, often with price drops exceeding $500. When evaluating these items, consider the total cost of ownership, which includes the price of the structure, potential installation labor, and necessary foundation work (Source: Lowe’s Project Guide).

Before purchasing a large structure, verify the following:

* Building codes: Check local zoning requirements to determine if your structure requires a permit or specific setbacks from property lines. * Foundation requirements: Hardtop gazebos often require a concrete pad or reinforced deck attachment; factor these costs into your total budget. * Assembly complexity: Many clearance-priced structures require professional assembly or significant time investment from the homeowner; ensure you have the necessary tools and labor available.

How should you store clearance patio furniture for the winter?

Proper storage is essential to extending the life of your discounted furniture through the winter months. If you live in an area with harsh winters, moisture and freezing temperatures can cause cracks in wicker, rust on metal, and degradation of wood finishes. Even if your furniture is labeled 'all-weather,' storing it correctly will prevent premature wear.

Actionable storage steps:

1. Clean thoroughly: Wash all surfaces with mild soap and water to remove salt, pollen, and debris that can attract mold. 2. Dry completely: Ensure all cushions and frames are bone-dry before covering to prevent mildew growth. 3. Use breathable covers: If you must store furniture outdoors, use high-quality, breathable covers rather than plastic tarps, which can trap moisture and cause damage. 4. Elevate items: If possible, store furniture on a raised surface or in a shed or garage to minimize contact with ground moisture.

Is it worth buying open-box or floor model furniture?

Buying floor models or open-box patio furniture can result in additional savings beyond advertised sale prices, but it requires a careful inspection process. Floor models have been exposed to the elements and frequent handling, which may result in minor cosmetic defects or missing hardware. Ask a store associate if the item is sold as-is or if it is eligible for a further discount due to its condition.

To safely buy floor models:

* Test all moving parts: Ensure hinges, folding mechanisms, and adjustable chair backs operate smoothly. * Check for surface damage: Look for deep scratches, dents, or signs of rust at the joints. * Request missing parts: If hardware is missing, ask if the store can provide replacements or offer a credit to purchase them separately. * Verify warranties: Confirm whether the manufacturer’s warranty still applies to floor models, as some policies exclude items that have been on display for an extended period.
