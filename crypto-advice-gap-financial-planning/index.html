Closing the crypto advice gap in financial planning

By Marcus Vance • 4 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

The crypto advice gap is the divide between clients holding digital assets and advisors who refuse to manage them. Learn how to bridge this gap.

Outline

  • Why are clients keeping crypto out of their advisory relationships?
  • How does crypto fit into long-term estate planning?
  • Should you change your approach to digital assets?
  • Is the "crypto-bro" stereotype outdated?

A crypto advice gap is the disconnect between the growing number of investors holding digital assets for the long term and the lack of professional guidance provided by traditional financial advisors. As digital assets become a permanent feature of household balance sheets, advisors who ignore them risk losing relevance and failing to provide comprehensive wealth management.

Recent surveys of high-net-worth individuals, including data from professional networks like Real Mamas of Crypto, indicate that a significant majority of crypto investors view assets like Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana as long-term core holdings rather than speculative day-trading vehicles (CoinDesk Indices, 2026). This shift in investor behavior marks the transition of cryptocurrency from a fringe asset class to a standard component of modern estate and inheritance planning.

Why are clients keeping crypto out of their advisory relationships?

The primary reason clients exclude their advisors from their crypto holdings is a perceived lack of competency or willingness from the advisor to engage with digital assets. Clients prioritize advisors who can simplify their financial lives by centralizing accounts and reducing administrative burdens. When an advisor refuses to acknowledge or manage a meaningful portion of a client’s net worth, the client is forced to manage that asset independently, creating a fractured financial picture.

According to industry analysis, the value of a modern wealth advisor lies in their ability to act as a central point of contact for all financial matters (CoinDesk Indices, 2026). When an advisor avoids the topic of digital assets, they inadvertently signal that they are not equipped to handle the complexities of a modern portfolio. This creates a "service gap" where the advisor’s refusal to engage with the asset class forces the client to look elsewhere for holistic planning, potentially leading to the termination of the advisory relationship.

How does crypto fit into long-term estate planning?

Cryptocurrency is increasingly being treated as a generational asset, with investors incorporating digital wallets and private key management into their formal estate plans. Unlike the volatile trading often portrayed in media, many long-term allocators are applying a "buy-and-hold" strategy, viewing these assets as wealth to be passed down to their children or beneficiaries.

For an advisor, this creates significant fiduciary challenges. If an advisor does not know a client holds substantial crypto assets, they cannot account for that risk in the client’s broader asset allocation or tax strategy. Furthermore, failing to provide guidance on the secure transfer of digital assets means the client is left to navigate complex custody issues, such as seed phrase storage and digital asset inheritance, without professional oversight. Advisors who facilitate these conversations add tangible value by mitigating the risk of lost assets or improper estate distribution.

Should you change your approach to digital assets?

Advisors must decide whether to integrate digital assets into their practice to maintain a competitive advantage in a changing market. Ignoring the asset class is a deliberate choice that carries the risk of client attrition as more households move crypto into their core financial planning.

To bridge the advice gap, consider these steps:

1. Audit your client base: Proactively ask clients about their exposure to digital assets during annual reviews to understand the full scope of their balance sheet. 2. Develop a custody policy: Determine how your firm will handle digital asset reporting, even if you are not actively trading or holding the assets yourself. Providing a reporting framework for these assets can be a significant value-add. 3. Educate on security and inheritance: Offer guidance on the mechanical aspects of digital asset ownership, such as the importance of secure storage and beneficiary access to private keys. 4. Integrate into financial plans: Use software or planning tools that allow you to include crypto holdings in a client’s net worth statement, ensuring that your advice on retirement, tax, and estate planning is based on complete financial data.

By treating cryptocurrency with the same professional rigor as traditional equities or fixed-income products, advisors can re-establish themselves as the primary authority in their clients' financial lives. The goal is not necessarily to become a crypto trader, but to become a competent navigator of the client’s entire financial ecosystem.

Is the "crypto-bro" stereotype outdated?

The stereotype of the crypto investor as a short-term, high-risk speculator is increasingly disconnected from the reality of how long-term investors behave. Data shows that many crypto-native professionals treat digital assets as a core, multi-year allocation, similar to how they treat traditional retirement accounts or index funds (CoinDesk Indices, 2026).

This shift is driven by the professionalization of the industry and the integration of blockchain technology into traditional finance. As institutional products become more accessible, the profile of the crypto investor has moved toward the cautious long-term allocator. Advisors who continue to rely on outdated stereotypes about crypto investors risk missing the opportunity to capture the loyalty of high-net-worth clients who are simply looking for professional guidance on a new, but legitimate, tool for wealth accumulation.
