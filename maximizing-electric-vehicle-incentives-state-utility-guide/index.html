Maximizing electric vehicle incentives: A state-by-state guide

By Groundwork Editorial • 4 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Federal EV credits have sunset. Learn how to secure funding for electric vehicle and charging infrastructure through utility-led programs and state grants.

An electric vehicle (EV) incentive is a financial program, such as a rebate, tax credit, or infrastructure grant, designed to offset the high upfront costs of transitioning fleets or personal vehicles to electric power. With federal programs sunsetting, utility-led initiatives have become the primary source of funding for EV adoption in the United States.

According to industry data, roughly 50% of all available EV incentive programs are currently funded or managed by local utility providers, rather than state or federal government agencies (Renewable Energy World, 2025). This shift means that your monthly utility provider is often your most valuable partner in managing the costs of fleet electrification.

Why are utilities the best source for EV funding? Utilities are the most effective source for EV funding because they provide faster access to capital, involve less bureaucracy than government agencies, and target specific infrastructure needs that directly impact your operations. Unlike federal grants that often require lengthy application processes and multi-year waiting periods, utility programs are designed to incentivize grid-load management and are frequently updated to reflect regional infrastructure requirements.

Utilities are incentivized to help you electrify because it increases electricity demand, which they can manage through smart-charging programs. Because these programs are regional, they are often tailored to the specific electrical grid capacity of your area, ensuring that the funding you receive covers the actual costs of local grid upgrades.

How do you find utility-based charging infrastructure programs? To find utility-based charging infrastructure programs, you must contact your local utility provider before signing any contracts for electrical work or equipment installation. Most utility-led programs offer "make-ready" infrastructure funding, which covers the costs of panel upgrades, conduit, and trenching—the most expensive parts of installing a charging station.

For example, Duke Energy, which operates across six states, offers a program that covers 100% of make-ready electrical costs for commercial customers, plus a fleet advisory bonus of up to $12,000 (Renewable Energy World, 2025). Similarly, National Grid in Massachusetts and New York offers up to 100% funding for make-ready infrastructure. If you proceed with installation before checking for these rebates, you may lose your eligibility, as many programs require pre-approval.

What happened to federal EV tax credits? Federal EV tax credits, specifically the 45W Commercial Clean Vehicle Credit and the 30C Charging Infrastructure Credit, were sunset following the passage of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act in 2025. This legislation effectively ended the national baseline of federal support for commercial fleet electrification and charging station development.

The 45W credit, which previously provided up to $40,000 for heavy equipment, ended for vehicles not under a binding contract by September 30, 2025. The 30C credit, which covered up to 30% of installation costs in specific census tracts, closed for projects installed after June 30, 2026. Because this federal layer of support is no longer available, you must pivot your financial strategy toward state-specific grants and utility-managed rebate programs to maintain a return on investment for your electrification projects.

How do you navigate state-level EV grant ecosystems? Navigating state-level EV grant ecosystems requires identifying programs that stack on top of your existing utility incentives. States like California maintain an extensive, multi-layered environment where agencies like PG&E, SCE, and LADWP operate their own programs independently of the state-level voucher systems, allowing businesses to layer benefits for a higher total coverage of costs.

To maximize your funding, follow this process: 1. Audit your current fleet and identify the specific charging infrastructure requirements, such as Level 2 or DC Fast Charging needs. 2. Contact your utility provider directly to request a list of active commercial electrification rebates and make-ready infrastructure programs. 3. Verify if your site location qualifies for "disadvantaged community" bonuses, which often increase subsidy percentages, such as the 100% make-ready coverage provided by Dominion Energy in Virginia. 4. Consult with your local dealer or installer to see if they participate in dual-track rebate programs, where the utility pays the vendor directly to reduce the upfront cost on your invoice. 5. Document all pre-approval requirements before breaking ground on any electrical site work.

Are there regional disparities in EV incentive availability? Yes, there are significant regional disparities in EV incentive availability because utility programs are tied to the specific service territories of the power companies. While major utilities like the Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA) offer broad, multi-state programs covering parts of seven states, other regions may have limited or no utility-backed incentives for commercial electrification.

For instance, programs in the Southeast, such as the TVA EnergyRight initiative, provide up to $2,000 per forklift for IC-to-electric conversions. In contrast, other regions may focus primarily on charging hardware rebates rather than equipment conversion. You must check the specific offerings of your utility provider, as geography remains the most significant predictor of how much funding you can secure for your transition to electric power.
