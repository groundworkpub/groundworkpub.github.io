What apple's new external link commission proposal means for you

By Groundwork Editorial • 3 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Apple has proposed a 15% commission on purchases made via external links. Learn how this impacts developers and what it means for your app subscriptions.

In This Guide

  • How does the proposed commission structure work?
  • Why is Apple changing its external payment policy?
  • How does this affect your experience as an app user?
  • What are the next steps for app developers and users?

An external link commission is a fee Apple charges developers for digital transactions initiated within an app that lead a user to complete a purchase on an external website. Following a long-standing legal dispute with Epic Games, Apple has submitted a proposal to the U.S. District Court of Northern California outlining a 15% fee structure for most standard applications.

This shift represents a significant change in how Apple monetizes the iOS ecosystem, moving away from a closed-loop system toward one where external web-based payments are permitted but taxed. According to the court filing, this proposal is a direct response to judicial requirements that Apple provide a clear commission framework for off-platform transactions.

How does the proposed commission structure work?

The proposed structure divides developers into tiers, with commission rates varying based on the size of the business and the type of content provided. For standard apps, the proposed fee is 15%. However, Apple has introduced specific discounts for developers who qualify for specialized programs or business categories.

1. Standard apps: 15% commission on external purchases. 2. Small business developers: 5% commission on external transactions. 3. Specialized partner programs: 10% commission for participants in the Video, News, and Mini Apps partner programs. 4. Subscription renewals: 10% commission for ongoing recurring payments.

These tiers are designed to reduce the financial burden on smaller entities while maintaining Apple's revenue stream from larger, high-revenue platforms that rely heavily on the iOS user base.

Why is Apple changing its external payment policy?

Apple is implementing these changes primarily due to ongoing legal pressure from the Epic Games litigation. For years, Apple required all digital goods to be purchased through its own In-App Purchase (IAP) system, which typically carried a 30% commission. The company argued that these fees are necessary to recoup investments in the tools, technology, and security services that maintain the App Store (TechCrunch, 2026).

However, courts have increasingly scrutinized these "walled garden" policies for potential anti-competitive behavior. By allowing external links, Apple is attempting to comply with judicial mandates while still capturing a portion of the revenue generated by its platform. Apple has drawn comparisons to Google Play’s fee structure, which charges 20% for standard apps and 15% for special programs, as a benchmark for its own pricing model.

How does this affect your experience as an app user?

For the average consumer, these changes may result in more options for managing subscriptions and making purchases outside of the native Apple interface. Previously, you were often restricted to the Apple payment system, which stores your payment information securely and streamlines the checkout process.

With external links, you might find that some apps now direct you to their own websites to complete a transaction. While this might offer developers more flexibility, it also means you may need to enter your credit card or payment information on third-party websites rather than using Apple Pay or saved App Store credentials. You should exercise caution when navigating to external sites and ensure you are using reputable payment gateways.

What are the next steps for app developers and users?

If you are a developer, you must monitor the court’s final ruling on these proposed percentages to understand how your revenue will be impacted. The court will evaluate whether these fees are reasonable and if they comply with previous injunctions regarding anti-steering provisions.

For users, the primary takeaway is awareness. As developers begin to implement external payment links, you will likely see a shift in how you subscribe to services. Always check if a subscription is managed through your Apple ID settings or through the developer’s own website, as managing cancellations and refunds will differ depending on where the purchase was initiated.
