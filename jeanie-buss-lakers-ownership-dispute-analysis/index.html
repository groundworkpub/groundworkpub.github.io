👉 Read the Full Study on Groundwork: The legal mechanics of the Buss family Lakers ownership dispute

A corporate governance dispute occurs when the controlling interest of an entity is challenged by minority shareholders or co-trustees, often resulting in legal deadlock. In the case of the Los Angeles Lakers, the conflict centers on whether the Buss family’s 17.8% stake in the organization can be sold to investors Bob Iger and Joshua Kushner without the consent of the controlling owner, Jeanie Buss.

At Groundwork, our analysis shows that ownership disputes within family trusts are governed by specific fiduciary duties and trust instruments that override informal family agreements. Understanding these mechanics is essential for grasping why Jeanie Buss’s legal team asserts that any unauthorized vote to sell the family’s interest is void.

Why does Jeanie Buss have the power to block the sale?

Jeanie Buss maintains authority over the sale of the family’s Lakers interest due to a 2017 court order that established her as the controlling shareholder. This legal designation provides her with specific protections that prevent other co-trustees from unilaterally disposing of the JAB Trust’s assets. According to legal counsel representing Buss, the trust requires that a minimum 15% ownership interest be maintained specifically to preserve her status as the controlling owner of the franchise.

When a trust holds significant assets like a professional sports franchise, the co-trustees are bound by a fiduciary duty to act in accordance with the trust's governing documents. If the current co-trustees—Jeanie, Janie, and Joey Buss—were to attempt a sale without consensus, they would risk violating the court-mandated structure. At Groundwork, our analysis of similar trust litigation suggests that courts typically prioritize the preservation of the controlling interest when explicit language in the trust instrument mandates that specific percentage thresholds be maintained to ensure operational stability.

What is the role of the JAB Trust in this dispute?

The JAB Trust is the legal vehicle through which the Buss family holds its 17.8% stake in Los Angeles Lakers, Inc. A trust is a fiduciary arrangement where a third party (the trustee) holds assets on behalf of beneficiaries. In this instance, the trust’s structure is the primary obstacle to the proposed sale to Iger and Kushner. Because the trust requires the co-trustees to vote shares in a manner that protects the controlling interest, any action taken to liquidate that stake without the consent of all necessary parties is legally vulnerable.

Legal experts emphasize that the fiduciary duty of a trustee is to the trust and its beneficiaries, not to external buyers. If the co-trustees act in a way that creates a 'breach of trust,' they become personally liable for the damages resulting from their actions. This creates a high barrier for any minority interest holder attempting to force a sale, as the legal consequences of defying a court order regarding trust assets are severe, including potential contempt of court charges.

How does the valuation of $12.5 billion affect the situation?

The reported valuation of the Los Angeles Lakers at $12.5 billion, following the purchase of Mark Walter’s majority stake by Iger and Kushner, significantly increases the stakes for all involved parties. High-valuation assets often trigger internal disputes because the immediate financial incentive for minority shareholders to 'cash out' can conflict with the long-term strategic interests of the controlling owner.

At Groundwork, our analysis indicates that when a franchise reaches a 'mega-valuation' threshold, the pressure to maintain control often outweighs the immediate liquidity benefits of a sale. For Jeanie Buss, the primary objective is not merely the financial gain from a sale, but the retention of control over the organization. When an asset is valued in the billions, the complexity of ownership structure—and the legal costs associated with defending that structure—often scale proportionally. Investors must be aware that in entities with family trust ownership, the price of the asset is secondary to the legal enforceability of the controlling interest.

What are the next steps for stakeholders?

For any party involved in a corporate or trust dispute, the process of resolution typically follows a rigorous legal path. First, verify the governing trust documents or shareholder agreements to determine the exact voting requirements for a sale. Second, ensure that any proposed transaction has the documented approval of the controlling interest holder. Third, if a dispute arises, seek a declaratory judgment from the court to define the rights and obligations of the co-trustees.

If you are navigating a similar situation, document every communication and ensure that all actions taken are consistent with the governing documents of the entity. Attempting to bypass a controlling owner in a trust-held asset is rarely successful without a clear legal pathway or a court-ordered modification of the trust. Always consult with legal counsel specializing in fiduciary law before proceeding with any transaction that could be construed as a breach of trust.
