A guide to mastering NYT Connections

Connections is a daily word association game from The New York Times that challenges players to categorize 16 seemingly unrelated words into four distinct groups of four based on shared characteristics. Designed by associate puzzle editor Wyna Liu, the game tests your ability to identify patterns across a variety of subjects, ranging from pop culture and geography to linguistic quirks and synonyms.

How to play Connections Connections is a logic-based puzzle where your primary goal is to identify four thematic groups of four words each from a grid of 16. To play, you select four words you believe share a common thread and submit them as a group. If your guess is correct, the words are removed from the board and the category is revealed. If your guess is incorrect, it counts as one of your four allowed mistakes. The game ends when you either successfully clear the board or exhaust your four attempts.

Understanding the difficulty levels Each of the four categories in a Connections puzzle is color-coded to indicate its relative difficulty level. The New York Times uses this system to help players gauge their progress and calibrate their strategy:

1. **Yellow:** These are typically the most straightforward categories, often involving simple synonyms or direct associations. 2. **Green:** These categories generally require a slightly higher level of critical thinking or specific topical knowledge. 3. **Blue:** These are often more complex, frequently involving wordplay, linguistic patterns, or obscure definitions. 4. **Purple:** These are traditionally the most difficult, often requiring lateral thinking, such as identifying words that share a hidden suffix or prefix.

Effective strategies for solving the grid Because multiple words on the board often appear to fit into more than one potential category, it is easy to get trapped by "red herrings"—words that seem like they belong together but are actually part of a different set. To improve your success rate, consider these evidence-based approaches:

* **Scan for obvious groupings first:** Start by identifying the most apparent category. Eliminating these four words narrows your search space, reducing the number of permutations for the remaining 12 words. * **Use the shuffle feature:** The game interface allows you to shuffle the grid. Rearranging the words can help break your brain’s tendency to see the same patterns over and over, allowing new associations to emerge. * **Look for linguistic patterns:** If you are stuck, look beyond the meaning of the words. Check for common suffixes, prefixes, or whether the words share a structural characteristic, such as being palindromes or having a specific number of syllables. * **Test your hypotheses carefully:** Since you only have four mistakes, use them sparingly. If you are unsure about a group, try to find a fifth word that might fit the category. If you find one, it suggests your current grouping is likely incorrect, and you should re-evaluate your logic.

Why word games are cognitively beneficial Engaging with daily puzzles like Connections is more than just a way to pass the time; it is a form of cognitive exercise. Research into neuroplasticity suggests that regularly challenging your brain with novel linguistic and logic tasks can help maintain cognitive flexibility. According to studies published in the *Journal of Cognitive Enhancement*, consistent engagement in mentally stimulating activities, such as word games, may help improve problem-solving skills and memory retention in adults. By forcing your brain to switch between different types of associations—from literal definitions to complex wordplay—you are effectively training your mind to process information more efficiently and recognize patterns in other areas of your daily life.
