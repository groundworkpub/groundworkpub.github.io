NYT Connections is a daily word-categorization game that challenges players to identify shared themes among 16 seemingly unrelated words. Puzzle #1161, released on August 15, features a specific mechanical pattern where the blue and purple categories require users to identify hidden words within larger terms. Understanding the underlying logic of these linguistic puzzles is essential for maintaining a high success rate.

How to approach the yellow category The yellow category in Connections represents the most straightforward group, typically focusing on direct synonyms or simple associations. For puzzle #1161, the theme identified was "exhausted." The four words corresponding to this category are "beat," "fried," "spent," and "worn out." When you encounter a puzzle of this type, begin by scanning for adjectives that describe a physical or mental state. Grouping these synonyms early reduces the complexity of the remaining grid, allowing you to focus on more abstract connections.

How to solve the green category Green categories usually involve slightly more nuance than yellow, often focusing on parts of a whole or functional components. In this instance, the theme was "segment." The correct grouping included "leg," "phase," "stage," and "stretch." To solve categories like this, analyze how the words function in a sentence. If they all describe a portion of a larger process or a physical journey, they are likely linked. If you find yourself stuck, try defining each word individually to see if a common noun or verb structure emerges.

How to identify hidden word categories The blue and purple categories in puzzle #1161 utilized a specific linguistic trick: words that contain other words at their suffix. This is a common pattern in Connections that rewards players who look beyond the definition of the word and examine its internal spelling. For the blue category, the theme was "ending in units of length." The words "chamomile" (mile), "Grinch" (inch), "hotfoot" (foot), and "lanyard" (yard) were selected. By breaking these down into their constituent suffixes, the hidden unit of measurement becomes clear. Always check the final three or four letters of a word if the primary definitions don't seem to form a coherent group.

How to handle complex linguistic patterns The purple category is frequently the most difficult, often relying on wordplay, puns, or, as seen here, synonyms for specific verbs. The theme was "ending in synonyms for follow secretly." The words included "beanstalk" (stalk), "eyeshadow" (shadow), "hightail" (tail), and "soundtrack" (track). This requires you to look at the suffix and then perform a second step of association—identifying that "stalk," "shadow," "tail," and "track" all describe the act of following someone discreetly. When a category feels impossible, list the suffixes of the remaining words. Often, the theme is hidden in the suffix rather than the root word itself.

Practical steps for consistent success To improve your performance in daily Connections games, follow this systematic process: 1. Identify the most obvious synonyms first to clear the board. 2. Look for grammatical similarities, such as all words being verbs or nouns. 3. If you remain stuck, isolate the last few letters of each word to check for embedded words or suffixes. 4. Use your four allotted mistakes to test theories, but be wary of "red herrings"—words that look like they belong in two categories. 5. If you are struggling with a specific group, walk away for a few minutes; fresh eyes often reveal linguistic patterns that were previously obscured by mental fatigue.
