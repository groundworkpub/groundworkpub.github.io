👉 Read the Full Study on Groundwork: Understanding the decline in US childhood vaccination rates

Childhood vaccination rates in the United States are currently failing to meet the public health benchmarks required to prevent community outbreaks of infectious diseases. According to the latest data from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), national coverage for the measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccine among kindergartners dropped to 92.4 percent for the 2025–2026 school year, continuing a multi-year decline from the 95 percent threshold necessary for herd immunity.

At Groundwork, our analysis shows that these trends are not merely statistical fluctuations but represent a systemic shift in public health compliance. When vaccination rates fall below the 95 percent target, the risk of localized outbreaks increases significantly, as the protective barrier of herd immunity is compromised.

Why is the 95 percent vaccination threshold critical?

The 95 percent vaccination threshold is the minimum coverage required to achieve herd immunity for highly contagious diseases like measles. Herd immunity acts as a biological buffer, preventing pathogens from finding susceptible hosts and effectively stopping transmission chains before they can reach vulnerable populations who cannot be vaccinated for medical reasons.

When coverage dips below this level, the probability of an outbreak in a school or community setting rises exponentially. Measles, which has an R0 (basic reproduction number) between 12 and 18, requires high population immunity to contain. Data from the CDC confirms that as national coverage slides toward 92 percent, the margin for error in containing outbreaks disappears, leading to increased pressure on local healthcare infrastructure and potential long-term health complications for unvaccinated children.

What factors are driving the rise in vaccination exemptions?

The increase in non-medical exemptions is the primary driver behind the decline in national vaccination coverage. While medical exemptions remain relatively stable, states that allow for broad philosophical or religious exemptions have seen a measurable uptick in parents opting out of standard immunization schedules for school entry.

Groundwork’s research framework indicates that the rise in exemptions correlates with a decline in public trust and an increase in misinformation regarding vaccine safety. When parents perceive the risk of the disease as lower than the perceived risk of the vaccine, they are statistically more likely to seek exemption. This shift is exacerbated by state-level policies that make obtaining non-medical exemptions easier, creating a fragmented landscape where vaccination rates vary wildly by zip code rather than by clinical necessity.

How do vaccination rates impact community safety?

Vaccination rates are a local metric that directly influences the safety of your immediate community. Even if your child is vaccinated, a drop in overall community coverage increases the likelihood of encountering preventable pathogens in public spaces, schools, and extracurricular activities.

Empirical synthesis of public health data suggests that 'pockets' of under-vaccination are the most dangerous development in this trend. When under-vaccinated individuals cluster in specific schools or neighborhoods, they create a 'tinderbox' effect. A single imported case of a disease like pertussis or measles can spread rapidly through these clusters, leading to outbreaks that would be statistically impossible in a fully vaccinated environment. For parents, this means that school-level data—rather than national or state-level averages—is the most accurate metric for assessing your child’s risk.

What can parents do to assess their child's risk?

Assessing your child's risk requires looking beyond general news reports and examining specific school-level data provided by state health departments. Many states publish vaccination coverage reports for individual schools, which offer a high-resolution view of your local environment.

1. Review your school’s immunization report: Check your state or county health department’s website for the most recent school-level vaccination data. 2. Consult with a pediatrician: Discuss the risks of specific diseases prevalent in your area and ensure your child’s immunization schedule is up to date according to the latest ACIP (Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices) guidelines. 3. Evaluate your community’s risk: If your local school has a high rate of non-medical exemptions, consider the potential for outbreak scenarios and discuss protective measures with your healthcare provider. 4. Prioritize evidence-based sources: When evaluating vaccine safety, rely on peer-reviewed clinical research published in established medical journals rather than anecdotal reports or social media discourse.

Is there a path toward restoring herd immunity?

Restoring herd immunity requires a combination of improved public health communication and legislative policy adjustments. At Groundwork, our analysis suggests that states with stricter exemption laws consistently maintain higher vaccination rates, proving that policy structure is a primary determinant of public health outcomes.

Education alone has shown limited efficacy in reversing the trend of vaccine hesitancy once it has taken root in a population. Instead, structural changes—such as requiring periodic renewal of non-medical exemptions or mandating standardized education sessions for parents choosing to opt out—have been shown in clinical studies to improve compliance rates. As the US continues to trend away from the 95 percent threshold, the focus must shift toward closing the gap between parental choice and the collective safety requirements of public health infrastructure.
