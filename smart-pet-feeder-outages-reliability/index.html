The risks of relying on cloud-connected smart pet feeders

By Groundwork Editorial • 3 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Smart pet feeders can fail during cloud outages, leaving pets hungry. Learn why relying on IoT for pet care is risky and how to implement manual backups.

In This Guide

  • Why smart feeder outages occur
  • The reality of local vs. cloud storage
  • How to mitigate the risk of smart home failure
  • Assessing the trade-off of convenience

A smart pet feeder is a device that automates the distribution of pet food based on schedules managed through a mobile application or cloud-based server. When these systems experience service outages, the automated feeding protocols can fail, potentially leaving pets without food while owners are away from home. Recent technical failures affecting major brands like Petlibro demonstrate that reliance on cloud connectivity introduces a single point of failure that can disrupt essential pet care.

According to reports from users on platforms like Reddit, widespread outages can cause smart feeders, water fountains, and automated litter boxes to disconnect from local networks entirely. While manufacturers often claim that devices retain local schedules, real-world performance during network-wide server failures suggests that these devices may not always operate as intended when the connection to the manufacturer’s cloud is severed (The Verge, 2024).

Why smart feeder outages occur

Smart pet feeders rely on a combination of local hardware and remote cloud infrastructure to function. The hardware manages the physical dispensing of food, but the programming, scheduling, and error reporting are often handled by a remote server. When a company experiences a server-side outage, your device may lose its ability to sync with the application or, in some cases, execute its internal clock.

Even if a device is designed for "offline mode," firmware bugs or power-cycle requirements can render that backup useless during an internet outage. If the device requires a cloud handshake to confirm its status, the hardware may enter a safe-mode state, effectively locking the dispenser to prevent potential malfunctions. This creates a scenario where the technology intended to provide convenience becomes a barrier to basic animal care.

The reality of local vs. cloud storage

Manufacturers frequently market devices as having "local storage" for feeding schedules. This means the device should theoretically dispense food even if the Wi-Fi or internet connection drops. However, in practice, many modern internet-of-things (IoT) devices perform frequent "check-ins" with manufacturer servers. If a firmware update is pushed during an outage or if the device encounters a synchronization error during a server crash, the local schedule can become corrupted or frozen.

Data from recent outages indicates that users are often left with no manual override when the digital interface fails. If the physical motor of the feeder is controlled exclusively through the app’s logic board, a software crash effectively turns the device into a paperweight until the manufacturer restores the cloud connection (The Verge, 2024).

How to mitigate the risk of smart home failure

To ensure your pet remains fed during technical outages, you should adopt a redundant approach to feeding. Relying solely on a smart device for essential health needs is a high-risk strategy. Consider the following steps to protect your pets:

1. Maintain a non-electric backup. Keep a manual gravity feeder or a simple bowl available for emergency use. 2. Use a local-only timer if possible. Some feeders allow for manual programming on the device itself without requiring an app or internet connection. 3. Arrange for human oversight. If you are going to be away for more than 24 hours, have a neighbor or pet sitter check on your pet regardless of the technology you use. 4. Test your feeder’s offline capabilities. Periodically turn off your Wi-Fi router while at home to observe if the device continues to dispense food as scheduled.

Assessing the trade-off of convenience

Smart pet products offer genuine benefits, such as portion control and remote monitoring, which can improve the health of pets with specific dietary needs. However, the trade-off is the introduction of technical debt into your daily routine. Before integrating smart technology into your pet’s life, evaluate the potential impact of a system failure. If a device failure could result in your pet missing a meal, it is essential to have a fail-safe plan that does not rely on software or internet connectivity. By treating smart feeders as a convenience rather than a critical infrastructure, you can enjoy the benefits of automation without compromising your pet’s well-being.
