How to optimize your dating profile photos for Gen Z preferences

By Alex Rivera • 4 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Should you include swimwear photos on your dating profile? Learn how Gen Z views photo etiquette and how to curate a profile that balances confidence and style.

Outline

  • Should you include swimwear or shirtless photos on your dating profile?
  • Why does Gen Z care about photo etiquette?
  • How to choose the right photos for your profile

A dating app profile is your digital introduction to potential partners, and recent data suggests that photo selection is a primary driver of how you are perceived by Gen Z users. While personal preference remains the most important factor in your profile design, understanding current trends regarding beach and swimwear photos can help you avoid potential misinterpretations.

Recent research from the dating app Hily surveyed 5,900 U.S. Gen Z and millennial users to understand how photo choices influence matches. The findings indicate that while confidence is generally appreciated, the timing and context of your photos—specifically regarding swimwear—can significantly impact how others view your intent and personality.

Should you include swimwear or shirtless photos on your dating profile?

Whether to include swimwear or shirtless photos on a dating profile is a personal choice, but you should be aware that a significant portion of Gen Z users associate these images with feelings of desperation or poor judgment. Data shows that 28 percent of Gen Z women and 34 percent of Gen Z men believe these photos are only acceptable during the summer months, and roughly one-third of Gen Z users view them as a signal of desperation rather than confidence.

If you choose to use these photos, consider the following trade-offs:

1. **The perception of confidence:** A majority of Gen Z users—62 percent of women and 74 percent of men—report that they view these photos as a sign of confidence. If your goal is to project self-assurance, these images can effectively communicate that trait to a large segment of your audience. 2. **The risk of being perceived as 'cringe':** A notable demographic, particularly among women, reports that they are less likely to 'like' a profile that features shirtless or bikini photos. This is often linked to a cultural desire to avoid appearing 'cringe' or overly eager in a digital space. 3. **Informed decision-making:** Nearly half of Gen Z users believe that seeing these photos helps them make a more informed decision about whether they are physically attracted to a potential match.

Why does Gen Z care about photo etiquette?

Gen Z's relationship with dating apps is heavily influenced by a digital-native upbringing that prioritizes authenticity and the avoidance of social faux pas. The fear of appearing 'cringe' or 'trying too hard' is a documented phenomenon that shapes how this generation presents themselves online. When you post a photo that is perceived as out of season or attention-seeking, you may inadvertently signal that you are not aligned with these social norms.

According to the Hily survey, 72 percent of young women and 56 percent of young men have never posted swimwear or shirtless photos on their profiles. This suggests that the 'default' setting for most users is to avoid these types of images, which may make them stand out more—either positively or negatively—when they do appear.

How to choose the right photos for your profile

To build a profile that balances attraction with social awareness, follow these steps to curate your gallery:

1. **Diversify your content:** Use a mix of photos that showcase your hobbies, your social life, and your personality. Relying solely on one type of photo—such as beach shots—limits the data you provide to potential matches. 2. **Consider the timing:** If you are determined to use a swimwear photo, consider the current season. While it may seem arbitrary, many users consciously or subconsciously judge the relevance of the photo to the current time of year. 3. **Prioritize high-quality, clear images:** Regardless of the subject matter, ensure your photos are well-lit and clear. A poorly lit, out-of-season photo is more likely to be perceived as 'low effort' than a high-quality photo that might be considered 'bold.' 4. **Reflect on your goal:** Are you looking for a long-term connection or a casual encounter? If your goal is a serious relationship, you may want to lead with photos that demonstrate your interests and lifestyle, using physical photos as a secondary element rather than a primary hook.

Ultimately, your dating profile should reflect who you are. While data provides a snapshot of general trends, it does not dictate your personal brand. If you feel confident and comfortable in your photos, that energy will often translate to the right audience, regardless of current trends.
