What the proposed MSCI index changes mean for your bitcoin-holding stocks

By Marcus Vance • 4 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

MSCI is proposing to exclude 'non-operating' companies from its indexes. Learn how this impacts bitcoin-heavy firms like Strategy and Metaplanet.

Outline

  • What is the new MSCI "non-operating company" proposal?
  • How does the two-step screening process work?
  • Why are Strategy and Metaplanet affected?
  • What are the implications for your portfolio?
  • Next steps for investors

An MSCI index exclusion is the removal of a publicly traded company from a benchmark stock index, which can trigger automatic selling by institutional investors. A new proposal by MSCI aims to exclude so-called "non-operating companies" from its Global Investable Market Indexes, a change that could directly impact major corporate bitcoin holders like Strategy and Metaplanet.

Institutional investors often track indexes like the MSCI ACWI IMI. When a company is removed, passive funds that mirror these indexes must sell their shares, potentially putting downward pressure on the stock price. According to MSCI, the goal of this proposal is to ensure that the companies included in their indexes are primarily focused on operational business activities rather than the accumulation of financial assets (MSCI, 2026).

What is the new MSCI "non-operating company" proposal?

The new MSCI proposal is a screening framework designed to filter out firms that derive their value primarily from holding financial assets rather than through traditional business operations. If a company fails to meet specific thresholds for operational intensity, it faces potential exclusion from the index provider's global equity benchmarks.

This proposal represents a shift in strategy for MSCI. Instead of targeting specific asset classes like cryptocurrency, the firm is creating a broader category of "non-operating companies." Under the proposed rules, a company is flagged if its operating assets do not constitute more than 50% of its total assets. Once a company falls below this threshold, it is subjected to a secondary, more rigorous financial screening process. This approach is intended to be asset-agnostic, though it effectively captures entities that utilize their balance sheets to accumulate large holdings of bitcoin or other commodities (MSCI, 2026).

How does the two-step screening process work?

The screening process is a systematic, data-driven approach that evaluates a company's financial structure. It is divided into two distinct phases: a core asset test and a secondary financial ratio analysis.

1. The core screen: This initial step evaluates the ratio of operating assets to total assets. If a company’s operating assets exceed 50% of its total balance sheet, it passes the test and remains eligible for index inclusion without further examination. 2. The exclusion screen: If a company fails the core screen, it undergoes an analysis of five specific financial ratios: operating asset intensity, expense intensity, cash flow, fair value intensity, and capital dependence. A company is deemed ineligible for index inclusion if it fails to meet at least four out of these five criteria.

This structure is designed to isolate firms that function more like investment vehicles than traditional operating corporations. By focusing on how a company generates value—specifically whether it creates value through business operations or through the appreciation of stored assets—MSCI seeks to maintain the purity of its investment indexes (MSCI, 2026).

Why are Strategy and Metaplanet affected?

Strategy and Metaplanet are affected because their current business models involve heavy allocation of capital toward bitcoin accumulation. Because these holdings represent a massive portion of their total assets, they struggle to meet the "operating assets" threshold required by the proposed MSCI rules.

Strategy has accumulated over 840,000 BTC, valued at approximately $53 billion, according to Bitcoin Treasuries (2026). Similarly, Tokyo-listed Metaplanet has acquired 43,000 BTC. Under the proposed criteria, these companies would likely fail the core 50% operating asset test. Because they rely on the appreciation of these holdings to drive shareholder value, they are highly likely to trigger the secondary exclusion screen. While the proposal does not explicitly name these companies, their financial structures align precisely with the definition of "non-operating companies" that MSCI intends to filter out of its investable universe (Coindesk, 2026).

What are the implications for your portfolio?

If you hold shares in companies targeted by these index changes, you may face increased volatility if the proposal is adopted. Institutional selling caused by index rebalancing can lead to temporary price drops, even if the underlying company remains financially healthy.

Investors should monitor the outcome of the consultation period, which ends on September 30, 2026. Any changes resulting from this proposal would not take effect until at least the November 2026 index review. If you are an investor in these specific stocks, you should assess whether your investment thesis relies on the company's long-term operational growth or its role as a proxy for bitcoin exposure. If your primary goal is bitcoin exposure, index exclusion may not alter the company's ability to hold its reserves, but it will significantly change the demand profile from institutional "passive" index funds.

Next steps for investors

1. Review your current exposure to companies with large treasury holdings of bitcoin or other non-operating assets. 2. Monitor the official MSCI announcement regarding the consultation results, expected in late 2026. 3. Consider the impact of potential institutional selling on the short-term price volatility of affected stocks. 4. Evaluate whether your long-term investment strategy is tied to the performance of the company's business operations or the underlying asset value of its treasury.
