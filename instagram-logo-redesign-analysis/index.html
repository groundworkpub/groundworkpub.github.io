What to know about the updated Instagram wordmark

👉 Read the Full Study on Groundwork: What to know about the updated Instagram wordmark

An Instagram wordmark is the stylized text logo that appears at the top of the app interface, distinct from the gradient camera icon. Instagram recently updated this script-based design for the first time in a decade to prioritize legibility and digital versatility across various screen sizes and device types.

According to Meta’s official design announcements, the new wordmark is part of a broader brand identity overhaul intended to modernize the platform's visual presence. While the iconic camera icon remains unchanged, the textual representation of the brand has been refined to be bolder and simpler, attempting to balance the brand’s history of handwritten script with contemporary design standards.

Why did Instagram change its wordmark?

Instagram updated its wordmark because the previous iteration had remained static for ten years, making it increasingly difficult to scale across diverse digital environments. Adam Mosseri, head of Instagram, stated that the refresh was necessary to keep the brand looking modern while maintaining a connection to its original, more personal roots.

Designers at Meta explored hundreds of variations, including all-caps layouts and camera-integrated lettering, before settling on a refined script. The goal was to signal individuality and personal expression—core tenets of the platform—while ensuring the logo remains crisp on everything from high-resolution smartphone screens to smaller, lower-density displays. By simplifying the strokes and adjusting the spacing, the design team aimed for a more readable, professional aesthetic.

What are the key elements of the new design?

The new wordmark features a hybrid of cursive and print lettering, moving away from the purely flowing script of the previous version. Some letters are connected, while others are intentionally disconnected to improve legibility. This design choice is intended to reduce the visual clutter that often occurs when fine-line script is scaled down for mobile application headers.

Beyond the primary wordmark, the update includes a broader ecosystem of assets. This includes the implementation of 'Instagram Sans,' a custom typeface, along with two supplementary fonts: 'Instagram Pen' and 'Instagram Mono.' These assets are being rolled out globally to provide a more consistent visual language across user profiles, ads, and interface elements. The design team has also indicated that future updates will include more restrained use of the platform's signature gradient and refined motion graphics, aiming for a more cohesive look by 2026.

How has the public responded to the redesign?

Public reception to the new Instagram wordmark has been mixed, with significant criticism focused on the legibility of specific characters. On platforms like Reddit and X (formerly Twitter), users have noted that the redesigned 's' and 'r' characters can be easily misread, leading some to jokingly suggest the logo reads as 'Instagzam.'

Design professionals on forums such as r/logodesign have critiqued the execution, describing the mixture of cursive and print as disjointed. Some users have characterized the update as a 'sidegrade'—a change that does not necessarily improve the original design—arguing that the new logo lacks the cohesive fluidity of its predecessor. Despite these criticisms, design changes of this nature are rarely intended to appease every user; rather, they are functional updates designed to improve performance and consistency for the company’s internal design systems.

What does this mean for your brand identity?

If you are managing a brand, the Instagram redesign serves as a practical lesson in the trade-offs between legacy branding and functional scalability. When refreshing a logo, the primary goal should always be legibility across the mediums where your audience spends the most time.

1. Audit your current assets: Identify which parts of your logo struggle at small scales, such as on mobile apps or social media profile icons. 2. Prioritize function over familiarity: While your audience may resist change, a logo that is illegible on a smartphone is a liability to your brand equity. 3. Test across devices: Before finalizing any design changes, verify that your wordmark remains clear and recognizable on both high-resolution and low-resolution displays. 4. Plan for a phased rollout: Large-scale branding changes, like Instagram’s, are best implemented gradually to ensure consistency across all digital and physical touchpoints.
