Understanding the break-even formula

When evaluating a mortgage refinance proposal, borrowers often focus only on the reduced monthly payment. Lower monthly payments do not automatically mean savings if you pay thousands in upfront fees.

**The simple break-even formula is: Total Upfront Closing Costs ÷ Monthly Payment Savings = Months required to break even.**

For a $300,000 loan, refinancing from 6.75% to 5.50% saves about $240/month. With $6,500 in closing costs, the break-even point is roughly 27 months. Stay in the home longer than that and you profit; move sooner and you lose.

What drives your break-even point

  • Closing costs typically run 2-5% of the loan amount
  • Rate drop, loan term reset, and how long you stay all matter
  • Debt-to-income ratio must be below 43% to qualify

When refinancing is not worth it

If you plan to move within two years, the math rarely works. Compare the net present value of total costs, not just the monthly payment.
