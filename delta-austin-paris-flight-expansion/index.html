Delta Air Lines’ new service from Austin-Bergstrom International Airport (AUS) to Paris-Charles de Gaulle (CDG) is a direct nonstop flight between the Texas capital and the French capital. This route, scheduled to launch on March 27, 2027, marks the airline's first long-haul international service from its growing Austin focus city.

According to aviation analytics firm Cirium, Delta has increased its total departures from Austin by 85% over the past three years. This expansion signals a strategic shift for the airline as it attempts to capture a larger share of the business and leisure travel market in a city traditionally dominated by Southwest Airlines.

When does the new Austin to Paris flight begin?

The new nonstop flight between Austin and Paris is scheduled to commence on March 27, 2027. This route will operate as a seasonal service, meaning flights will run daily during the peak summer travel months before winding down in the autumn, according to Delta's official route announcement.

Travelers should note the following details regarding the service:

1. **Route:** Austin (AUS) to Paris-Charles de Gaulle (CDG). 2. **Frequency:** Daily service during the summer season. 3. **Aircraft:** The route will be serviced by the Airbus A330-900neo, an aircraft designed for long-haul efficiency. 4. **Cabin configuration:** The plane features 29 Delta One suites, 28 Premium Select recliners, 56 Comfort+ extra legroom seats, and 168 main cabin seats.

Why is Delta targeting Austin for long-haul growth?

Delta is treating Austin as a "focus city" rather than a traditional hub, meaning the airline is concentrating resources in a high-growth market where it does not maintain a massive connecting operation. By launching a long-haul route from a non-hub city, Delta is betting on the high demand for international travel among Austin’s tech and business sectors.

This move is part of a broader infrastructure investment at AUS. Delta has committed to expanding its footprint at the airport, including plans for 15 additional gates and a second, larger Sky Club lounge. These physical improvements are designed to increase passenger loyalty and provide a premium experience that differentiates Delta from the airport’s primary carrier, Southwest Airlines.

How does this affect Austin's international connectivity?

The addition of the Paris route brings Austin’s nonstop European destination count to four. While other carriers operate transatlantic flights from Austin, Delta’s entry is notable because it represents the first U.S. carrier to operate a nonstop flight between Austin and Paris.

For the average traveler, this competition is generally positive. Increased frequency and more airline options on international routes historically lead to more competitive pricing and better schedule flexibility. However, because this is a seasonal route, travelers should plan their European itineraries around the spring and summer availability windows to ensure they can secure a nonstop seat.

What should you do if you are planning to fly this route?

If you are considering booking this flight for your 2027 travel, you should monitor Delta’s booking engine as the launch date approaches. Because this is a high-profile route, award seat availability may be limited during the initial rollout.

1. **Set fare alerts:** Use tools like Google Flights to track price fluctuations for AUS to CDG once the schedule is fully loaded into reservation systems. 2. **Check seasonal dates:** Since the service is seasonal, verify the exact end date for the autumn schedule before finalizing hotel bookings in Paris. 3. **Evaluate cabin class:** With the A330-900neo, weigh the cost-to-value ratio of Delta Premium Select, which offers more space than main cabin but at a lower price point than the Delta One lie-flat suites. 4. **Utilize loyalty points:** If you hold Delta SkyMiles, check for "saver" level award space early, as these seats are often released months in advance of the first flight date.
