How to fill finished concrete floor gaps along the perimeter

By David Sterling • 4 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Sealing concrete floor gaps prevents air leaks, pest entry, and moisture migration. Use flexible polyurethane sealants for a durable, airtight finish.

Outline

  • Why must you seal perimeter concrete gaps?
  • Can you use standard concrete patch products?
  • How to seal perimeter gaps step-by-step
  • How to handle pipe penetrations through the slab
  • Finishing the area with trim

A concrete floor gap is the physical separation between a slab-on-grade floor and the adjacent foundation wall, which can lead to air infiltration, pest entry, and moisture issues if left unsealed. Sealing these gaps is a critical step in building science to maintain the integrity of your home’s thermal envelope and vapor barrier.

According to building science experts, the primary goal when addressing these gaps is to create an airtight and moisture-resistant seal that allows for the natural expansion and contraction of the concrete slab (Green Building Advisor, 2026). Because concrete is a porous material that reacts to temperature fluctuations, using rigid fillers can lead to cracking. Instead, you must use flexible, high-performance sealants that accommodate movement while preventing the migration of soil gases like radon.

Why must you seal perimeter concrete gaps?

The perimeter gap between a slab and the foundation wall acts as a conduit for unwanted air, moisture, and potential pests. If your vapor barrier was compromised during the concrete pour—a common occurrence where the plastic is pulled down or torn—the gap serves as a direct bridge between your living space and the earth beneath the foundation.

Unsealed gaps allow for stack effect, where warm air escapes through the top of the house and draws cold, damp air from the crawlspace or soil through these perimeter voids. Furthermore, sealing these areas is essential for radon mitigation. The EPA notes that sealing all entry points, including slab-to-wall gaps, is a foundational step in reducing indoor radon concentrations (EPA, 2023). By creating a continuous seal, you protect your indoor air quality and improve the energy efficiency of your home.

Can you use standard concrete patch products?

Standard concrete patch products are generally unsuitable for perimeter gaps because they are designed for surface repair rather than joint sealing. Many hardware store concrete fillers cure to a rigid, brittle state. When the concrete slab inevitably shifts due to seasonal temperature changes, a rigid patch will crack, negating the work you just performed.

Instead, you should look for polyurethane-based sealants or high-quality elastomeric caulks. Products like Sikaflex or similar high-grade polyurethane joint sealants are engineered specifically for concrete. These products remain flexible after curing, meaning they can stretch and compress as the concrete moves without losing their bond to the foundation wall or the slab edge. Always check the manufacturer's technical data sheet to ensure the product is rated for interior use and has low or zero volatile organic compound (VOC) emissions to ensure it does not negatively impact indoor air quality.

How to seal perimeter gaps step-by-step

To effectively seal the perimeter, you must ensure the area is clean, dry, and free of debris. Following a systematic approach prevents future failures and ensures a long-lasting barrier.

1. Clear the gap: Use a vacuum to remove dust, loose concrete chips, and debris from the gap. If there is significant debris, the sealant will not adhere properly to the concrete. 2. Install backer rod: For gaps wider than 1/4 inch, insert a closed-cell polyethylene backer rod. This prevents the sealant from "three-sided adhesion," which occurs when the sealant bonds to the bottom of the gap as well as the two sides. Three-sided adhesion restricts the sealant's ability to stretch, leading to premature failure. 3. Apply the sealant: Use a high-quality, paintable polyurethane caulk. Apply it with a caulking gun, ensuring you fill the gap completely without creating large air bubbles. 4. Tool the joint: Use a putty knife or a specialized caulk tool to smooth the sealant flush with the floor. This provides a clean aesthetic that will be easily covered by your baseboards or decorative trim. 5. Allow to cure: Follow the manufacturer's instructions for cure time before installing trim or applying floor finishes.

How to handle pipe penetrations through the slab

Penetrations for hydronic heating or plumbing represent the most vulnerable points in your slab’s vapor barrier. Because these pipes often move independently of the concrete, they require a specialized approach to maintain a seal.

When sealing around pipes, you need a sealant that can handle the vibration and heat expansion of the pipe itself. Polyurethane sealants are again the preferred choice here. If the gap around the pipe is large, you may need to use a spray foam specifically designed for fire-stopping or air-sealing, provided it is compatible with the pipe material. However, for most small gaps, a bead of flexible polyurethane sealant applied around the circumference of the pipe is sufficient. Ensure the sealant bridges the gap between the pipe and the concrete, creating a continuous, airtight seal that prevents moisture from wicking up along the pipe surface.

Finishing the area with trim

Once the sealant has fully cured, you can install your baseboards or decorative trim. Because the sealant is flexible, the trim will sit against a stable, sealed surface. If the gap is exceptionally wide and you are concerned about the trim covering the seal, you can use a wider profile baseboard or add a decorative shoe molding. This ensures that the aesthetic finish of your room remains intact while the structural integrity of your air barrier is preserved behind the walls.
