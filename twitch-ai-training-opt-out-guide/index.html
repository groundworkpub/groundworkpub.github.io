How to opt out of Twitch AI training

By Groundwork Editorial • 3 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Learn how to opt out of Twitch AI training to stop Amazon from using your streams and videos to develop generative artificial intelligence models.

In This Guide

  • How to disable AI training for your Twitch channel
  • What are the limitations of this opt-out setting?
  • Why was AI training enabled by default?
  • Is your content being used by other companies?

A Twitch AI training opt-out is a privacy setting that allows content creators to prevent their live streams, clips, and recorded videos from being processed by Amazon’s generative artificial intelligence models. Because Amazon owns Twitch, your broadcasted content is considered data that the parent company may use to develop its machine learning systems unless you take specific steps to restrict that access.

Recent reports indicate that more than 16,000 creators have expressed opposition to the default inclusion of their content in these training datasets (Wired, 2024). This widespread concern highlights a significant shift in the digital creator economy, where the ownership and secondary use of user-generated content have become central points of contention between platforms and their users.

How to disable AI training for your Twitch channel

To stop Twitch from using your content to train its generative AI models, you must manually toggle the setting within your account dashboard. Follow these steps to secure your data:

1. Sign in to your Twitch account via a web browser or the mobile application. 2. Click on your profile avatar located in the top-right corner of the screen. 3. Navigate to the **Settings** menu. 4. Select the **Security and Privacy** tab from the provided options. 5. Locate the section labeled **Generative AI Training**. 6. Toggle the switch to **Disabled** to opt out of the program.

Once you have completed these steps, your future content will be excluded from the datasets used specifically for training Amazon’s generative AI systems. It is important to note that this action does not delete your existing content, nor does it necessarily remove data that may have already been ingested prior to you changing the setting.

What are the limitations of this opt-out setting?

Disabling the generative AI training toggle does not stop Twitch or Amazon from using your content for other platform-related features. Twitch explicitly states that they may still process your streams for tools designed to facilitate platform growth, monetization, and safety (Twitch Privacy Notice, 2024).

These permitted uses include, but are not limited to, real-time sponsorship assistance, viewer recommendation algorithms, and community moderation tools like AutoMod. While these features are also AI-powered, the company distinguishes them from the broader generative AI training models used for commercial product development. If you are concerned about how your data is used for these specific platform features, you should review the full Twitch Privacy Notice to understand the scope of their data collection policies.

Why was AI training enabled by default?

Twitch leadership has acknowledged that the setting was enabled by default because they believed a voluntary, opt-in system would result in insufficient participation for meaningful data collection (Twitch Product Update, 2024). By defaulting creators into the program, the platform ensured a larger, more diverse dataset for training purposes.

This approach mirrors a broader industry trend where major technology firms treat publicly available internet content as a common resource for model training. Critics argue that this practice bypasses individual consent, effectively using the labor of creators to build products that may eventually compete with or replace their own services. While Twitch maintains that this is standard industry practice, the lack of transparency regarding when this training began remains a primary point of friction for the creator community.

Is your content being used by other companies?

While opting out of the Twitch-specific setting protects you from Amazon’s internal generative AI training, it does not prevent third-party scrapers or other AI developers from harvesting your content from the public web (Wired, 2024). Because Twitch streams are publicly accessible, they are often subject to automated data collection by entities outside of Amazon’s control.

If you are a creator concerned about the long-term usage of your intellectual property, consider limiting the availability of your VODs (Video on Demand) to subscribers only. While this is not a comprehensive solution against all forms of data scraping, it adds a layer of friction that can prevent automated bots from accessing your archives.
