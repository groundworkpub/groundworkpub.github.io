Samsung is reportedly developing a new pair of over-ear wireless headphones, internally referred to as the "Galaxy H1," which would mark the company's first entry into this specific form factor since 2015. Evidence found within the code of Samsung’s Galaxy Wearable app suggests that the product is in active development, potentially positioning the brand to compete directly with premium over-ear options like the Apple AirPods Max or Sony WH-1000XM5.

According to reports from SamMobile, the potential release window for these headphones extends into 2027, indicating that the product is still in the early stages of the hardware lifecycle. While Samsung has focused heavily on earbuds like the Galaxy Buds series following their acquisition of Harman—the parent company of AKG and JBL—the move toward over-ear headphones represents a strategic pivot to capture the premium wireless audio market.

What evidence exists for the Galaxy H1?

The existence of the Galaxy H1 was identified through strings of code and UI icons discovered within the latest version of the Samsung Galaxy Wearable application. These assets include connection status icons that explicitly depict an over-ear headphone design, which differentiates them from the in-ear or canal-type designs typically used for the Galaxy Buds lineup. While software code is not a guarantee of a final retail product, it serves as a primary indicator of hardware projects under internal testing.

Samsung has not officially confirmed these plans, but the inclusion of these icons in a consumer-facing app suggests that the company is preparing the software infrastructure necessary to manage an over-ear device. This development follows a long gap in Samsung’s catalog, as the company has not released a branded over-ear headset since the "Level On" series launched nearly a decade ago.

Why is Samsung returning to over-ear headphones?

Samsung is re-entering the over-ear market to address a gap in its ecosystem and compete with high-end audio manufacturers who currently dominate the premium wireless headphone segment. By leveraging its ownership of Harman, Samsung has the engineering expertise to integrate proprietary audio technologies, such as advanced active noise cancellation (ANC) and spatial audio, which are now standard expectations for headphones in the $300 to $500 price range.

Industry data from analysts at Counterpoint Research shows that while true wireless stereo (TWS) earbuds account for the bulk of audio sales, the over-ear wireless segment maintains higher average selling prices and stronger brand loyalty among audiophiles and frequent travelers. By launching a premium over-ear model, Samsung aims to keep users within its "Galaxy" ecosystem, ensuring that features like seamless device switching and high-resolution codec support remain exclusive to its phones and tablets.

How will the Galaxy H1 compete with existing market leaders?

To succeed, the Galaxy H1 will likely need to offer deeper integration with the Android ecosystem than third-party competitors currently provide. Currently, the premium market is dominated by the Apple AirPods Max, which relies on the H1/H2 chip for spatial audio and instant pairing, and the Sony WH-1000XM series, which is widely considered the industry benchmark for noise cancellation performance.

Samsung’s competitive advantage may lie in its ability to offer "Galaxy AI" features, which have become a core pillar of their recent smartphone marketing. Potential features could include real-time language translation, adaptive sound profiles based on environment, and deeper integration with the SmartThings platform for cross-device audio handoff. If Samsung can successfully port its software ecosystem advantages to a high-fidelity over-ear form factor, it could offer a compelling alternative for users who are already invested in Samsung devices but want more than what standard earbuds provide.

What should you do if you are looking for new headphones?

If you are currently in the market for premium over-ear headphones, you should base your purchase on current hardware availability rather than waiting for future rumors. Technology cycles for audio equipment are lengthy, and a 2027 release window for the Galaxy H1 is far enough away that it should not influence your immediate purchasing decisions.

1. Evaluate your current ecosystem: If you use a Samsung phone, consider headphones that offer high-resolution codec support like LDAC or aptX Adaptive to ensure the best audio quality. 2. Assess your primary use case: Determine if you need headphones primarily for noise cancellation during travel or for high-fidelity home listening. 3. Research current benchmarks: Compare the Sony WH-1000XM5 and Bose QuietComfort Ultra, which currently set the standard for battery life, comfort, and ANC performance. 4. Wait for official announcements: Avoid pre-ordering or delaying upgrades based on leaks, as technical specifications and release dates are subject to change during the development process.
