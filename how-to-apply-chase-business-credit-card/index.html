How to complete a Chase business credit card application

By Groundwork Editorial • 4 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Applying for a Chase business credit card requires proof of business activity and a strong personal credit profile. Learn the steps and 5/24 rule requirements.

A Chase business credit card application is a formal request for a line of credit issued to your business entity, which may include a sole proprietorship, partnership, or limited liability company. Chase evaluates your business's financial viability alongside your personal credit history to determine your eligibility for credit products like the Ink Business series.

Approximately 33% of U.S. workers engage in some form of freelance or gig work, according to data from Upwork, which makes many individuals eligible for business credit cards even without a formal corporate structure. Applying for these cards allows you to separate personal and business expenses, which is a foundational step in professional financial management.

What are the eligibility requirements for a Chase business card? To qualify for a Chase business card, you must demonstrate that you operate a business, even if it is a small-scale operation like a side hustle or freelance consulting. You do not need to be incorporated as an LLC or C-Corp; sole proprietorships are widely accepted provided you have a legitimate business purpose and potential for revenue.

Chase requires applicants to provide information about their business structure, annual revenue, and tax identification. If you are a sole proprietor, you can typically use your Social Security Number (SSN) as your tax ID. The bank assesses your personal creditworthiness as the primary guarantor of the debt, meaning your personal credit score remains the most significant factor in the approval process.

How does the Chase 5/24 rule affect your application? Chase’s 5/24 rule is an internal policy stating that you will likely be denied for a new credit card if you have opened five or more personal credit card accounts from any issuer within the previous 24 months. This rule is designed to limit exposure to applicants who appear to be primarily seeking sign-up bonuses rather than long-term credit relationships.

Importantly, most business credit cards do not appear on your personal credit report, according to analysis by The Points Guy and other financial monitors. Because these accounts are not tracked on your personal credit file, opening a new business card typically does not increase your count toward the 5/24 limit. However, you must be under the 5/24 limit at the time of your application to receive approval for a new Chase card.

What information do you need to provide on the application? Applying for a business card requires specific financial data that reflects your business's current operations and future potential. You should have the following details prepared before you begin the process:

1. Legal business name: If you are a sole proprietor, this is your legal name. 2. Business address: The physical location where your business operates. 3. Business tax ID: This is your Employer Identification Number (EIN) if you have one, or your Social Security Number if you are a sole proprietor. 4. Business structure: Identify whether you are a sole proprietorship, LLC, corporation, or partnership. 5. Annual business revenue: This is the total gross income your business generates annually. If your business is new, provide a realistic estimate based on your current trajectory. 6. Years in business: The duration of time you have been operating your business entity. 7. Number of employees: Include yourself in this count if you are the sole operator.

How to navigate the application process step by step Following a systematic approach can help ensure your application is processed efficiently. If you are already a Chase personal banking customer, logging into your account before starting the application will pre-fill some of your personal data, reducing the likelihood of entry errors.

1. Choose your product: Evaluate the specific rewards structure of the Ink Business Cash, Ink Business Unlimited, or Ink Business Preferred cards based on your primary spending categories. 2. Access the application: Navigate to the official Chase business credit card page and select the 'Apply Now' button. 3. Enter business details: Input your business information accurately. Ensure that your revenue projection is consistent with your business model. 4. Provide personal details: Even though this is a business application, you must provide your personal contact information and financial details, as you are the individual personally guaranteeing the debt. 5. Review and submit: Verify all information for accuracy. Typos in your EIN or SSN can lead to automatic denials or requests for additional documentation. 6. Monitor the status: If your application is not instantly approved, you may receive a status update via email or be asked to provide further proof of business existence, such as a business license or tax document.

What happens if you are not instantly approved? It is common for business credit card applications to go to 'pending' status rather than receiving an immediate approval. This often happens because the bank needs to manually verify the business information or conduct a more thorough review of the applicant's credit profile.

If you receive a pending notice, do not reapply immediately. Instead, wait for communication from Chase. You may be contacted by their reconsideration line to provide additional details about your business, such as a description of your services or evidence of your anticipated revenue. Being prepared to explain your business model clearly and calmly can significantly improve your chances of overturning a preliminary denial.
