What Netflix closing its gaming studios means for your subscription

By Alex Rivera • 3 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Netflix is closing two gaming studios, Night School and Moonloot, to pivot toward TV-based party games. Learn how this shift affects your subscription.

Netflix is restructuring its internal gaming division by shutting down two development studios, Night School Studio and Moonloot Games, as it pivots toward a new strategy focused on party games and TV-streamed titles. A gaming studio closure is a strategic decision by a publisher to cease operations at a specific development house to reduce overhead or refocus resources on different genres or platforms.

According to reports from Game File and Variety, this consolidation marks a significant shift away from the company's initial mobile-first gaming strategy. While Netflix has invested heavily in prestige mobile titles since 2021, the company is now prioritizing games that can be played on television screens, likely to increase user engagement within the living room environment rather than just on mobile devices.

Why is Netflix shutting down these specific studios? Netflix is closing Night School Studio and Moonloot Games to align its internal development capacity with its new focus on party-oriented and TV-compatible experiences. Night School Studio, known for the critically acclaimed Oxenfree, was the company’s first high-profile acquisition in the gaming space, while the Helsinki-based Moonloot Games was established in 2022 to build original intellectual property.

The decision reflects the broader volatility of the gaming industry, where companies often acquire studios to experiment with new content types, only to pivot when engagement data suggests a different direction. By streamlining its studio footprint, Netflix is likely attempting to cut costs while doubling down on genres that integrate more seamlessly into their existing streaming ecosystem, such as party games that leverage the "Netflix on TV" user experience.

How does this affect the games you currently play? For most subscribers, the closure of these studios will not immediately disrupt your access to existing titles currently available in the Netflix Games library. Games previously released by these studios, such as the Oxenfree series or recent Black Mirror-themed content, typically remain available on the platform unless the company specifically decides to pull them due to licensing or maintenance costs.

However, the closure signals a clear end to new, experimental projects from these specific teams. If you enjoyed the narrative-heavy, indie-style games produced by Night School Studio, you should not expect sequels or new titles from those creators under the Netflix banner. Instead, future additions to the platform are likely to be developed by third-party partners or internal teams focused on more casual, multiplayer-friendly experiences.

What is the new Netflix gaming strategy? Netflix is shifting its focus toward games that can be streamed directly to televisions, moving away from the mobile-exclusive model that characterized its initial entry into the gaming market. This pivot is designed to capture the attention of users already using the Netflix app on their smart TVs, gaming consoles, and streaming sticks.

Data suggests that while mobile gaming is a massive market, Netflix's competitive advantage lies in its massive installed base of TV viewers. By transitioning to TV-streamed titles, the company aims to turn the Netflix app into a centralized entertainment hub where you can toggle between watching a film and participating in a quick, interactive party game without needing to download a separate app or switch devices.

Should you expect more changes to Netflix games? It is highly probable that Netflix will continue to adjust its gaming portfolio based on real-time engagement metrics, as the company is still in a "testing" phase regarding how gaming fits into a video-streaming subscription. You should expect the platform to prioritize high-engagement, low-friction titles over complex, narrative-driven games that require long-term commitment from the player.

If you are a casual gamer, this shift may actually result in a more accessible library of games that are easier to pick up and play for short durations. If you prefer deep, story-based experiences, the platform may become less appealing over time as the company moves toward more "snackable" content. To stay updated, keep an eye on the "Games" tab within your Netflix app, as the company frequently rotates its library based on these strategic pivots.
