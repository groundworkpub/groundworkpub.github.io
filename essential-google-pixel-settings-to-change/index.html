Essential settings to change on your new Google Pixel

By Alex Rivera • 4 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Learn how to optimize your Google Pixel by adjusting settings for battery life, privacy, and performance. Follow these steps to customize your device usage.

Outline

  • Should you disable the 'At a Glance' widget?
  • How do you improve battery life by disabling 'Now Playing'?
  • Why should you customize your quick tap gestures?
  • Are you managing your 'Adaptive Connectivity' settings correctly?
  • How to optimize your display for battery and comfort

A Google Pixel settings adjustment is the process of modifying default software configurations to optimize your device’s battery life, privacy, and user interface. While Google’s stock Android experience is generally clean, several factory-default settings are designed for discoverability rather than performance or user preference.

Research indicates that over 60% of smartphone users rarely explore deep system settings, often missing out on privacy controls and power-saving features that could extend their device's utility (Source: Pew Research Center). By spending ten minutes adjusting these specific parameters, you can improve how your Pixel functions daily.

Should you disable the 'At a Glance' widget?

The 'At a Glance' widget is a non-removable information hub on the Pixel home screen that displays weather, calendar alerts, and travel updates. While convenient for some, it occupies significant screen real estate and consumes background data to fetch real-time updates that you may not need at all times.

To manage this, long-press the widget and select 'Customize.' You can toggle off specific categories like 'Commute' or 'Package delivery' to reduce clutter. If you prefer a cleaner aesthetic, reducing the number of active alerts ensures the widget remains functional without overwhelming your home screen. According to Google’s own support documentation, restricting these data feeds can marginally reduce background battery drain over time.

How do you improve battery life by disabling 'Now Playing'?

'Now Playing' is a feature that identifies music playing in your environment and displays the song title on your lock screen. While technologically impressive, it requires the device’s microphone to constantly listen for audio signatures, which contributes to increased battery consumption and potential privacy concerns for some users.

To disable or refine this, navigate to Settings > Sound & vibration > Now Playing. If you find the battery drain impacts your daily usage, turn the feature off entirely. If you prefer to keep it, ensure 'Now Playing History' is managed so that your device isn't storing an unnecessary log of your environmental audio history. Data from Android Authority suggests that disabling persistent background listening features can save approximately 2-3% of battery life over an eight-hour period.

Why should you customize your quick tap gestures?

Quick Tap is a gesture-based shortcut that allows you to trigger specific actions by double-tapping the back of your phone. By default, this may be set to take a screenshot or do nothing at all, which is often inefficient if you frequently use other tools like the flashlight or your digital wallet.

1. Open Settings and go to System. 2. Tap on Gestures. 3. Select 'Quick Tap to start actions'. 4. Choose a function that matches your workflow, such as 'Toggle flashlight' or 'Open specific app'.

Customizing this allows you to bypass multiple screen taps, saving time during routine tasks. This feature utilizes the phone’s accelerometer and gyroscope, meaning it does not require additional software permissions to function, making it both a secure and efficient customization.

Are you managing your 'Adaptive Connectivity' settings correctly?

'Adaptive Connectivity' is an automated feature designed to switch between 5G and LTE to preserve battery life based on your current network demand. However, in areas with inconsistent 5G coverage, this switching process can cause the phone to constantly search for a signal, leading to significant battery drain and intermittent connection drops.

If you notice your phone is frequently warm or losing battery rapidly while in poor coverage zones, you should consider disabling this feature. Navigate to Settings > Network & internet > Adaptive connectivity. By forcing your device to stay on a stable 4G/LTE connection in weak signal areas, you can often achieve more consistent data speeds and better battery performance, as corroborated by technical testing from XDA Developers.

How to optimize your display for battery and comfort

Your Pixel’s display settings, specifically 'Smooth Display' and 'Always-on Display,' significantly impact your battery longevity. 'Smooth Display' boosts the refresh rate to 90Hz or 120Hz for fluid scrolling, which is visually pleasing but power-intensive.

To adjust these: 1. Go to Settings > Display. 2. Tap 'Smooth Display' to toggle it off if you prioritize battery life over fluid animations. 3. Check 'Lock screen' settings to manage 'Always-on display.'

For most users, keeping 'Always-on display' active is useful, but ensuring it is set to 'Always show time and info' rather than including complex weather or notification previews can save power. A study by DisplayMate confirms that higher refresh rates and constant screen illumination are the two largest contributors to mobile display power consumption. By balancing these settings, you maintain a high-quality visual experience without sacrificing the ability to get through a full day on a single charge.
