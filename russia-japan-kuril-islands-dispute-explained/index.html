A territorial dispute is a disagreement over the possession or control of land between two or more political entities. The Kuril Islands dispute is a long-standing sovereignty conflict between Japan and Russia regarding a chain of islands located between the Kamchatka Peninsula and Hokkaido. This disagreement remains a primary obstacle to a formal peace treaty between the two nations following World War II.

Recent tensions escalated in August 2026 when Russian President Vladimir Putin made his first-ever visit to the islands, specifically visiting Iturup, one of the four contested territories. Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi officially condemned the visit, labeling it "absolutely unacceptable" and reaffirming that the islands are "inherently Japanese territory" (CNBC, 2026). This diplomatic friction underscores the ongoing challenges in regional geopolitics.

What is the history of the Kuril Islands dispute?

The Kuril Islands, known in Japan as the Northern Territories, were occupied by the Soviet Union during the final stages of World War II. While Japan signed the 1951 Treaty of San Francisco, in which it renounced claims to the Kuril chain, the Japanese government maintains that the four southernmost islands—Iturup, Kunashir, Shikotan, and the Habomai rocks—do not fall under that renunciation because they are inherent Japanese territory (Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan, 2026). Russia, as the successor state to the Soviet Union, asserts sovereignty based on the outcome of the war and subsequent administrative control.

Why are the islands strategically important?

The Kuril Islands serve as a critical buffer between the Sea of Okhotsk and the Pacific Ocean. Control over these islands provides Russia with year-round access to the Pacific, as the surrounding waters remain ice-free even in winter. Furthermore, Russia maintains a significant military presence on Iturup, which serves as a strategic outpost for monitoring maritime traffic and projecting power in the North Pacific region (Institute for the Study of War, 2026). The islands are also rich in marine resources, specifically high-value fisheries that contribute to the local economy.

How does this dispute affect Japan-Russia relations?

The territorial disagreement has effectively prevented the signing of a formal post-WWII peace treaty between Tokyo and Moscow for over eight decades. Although a 1956 joint declaration technically ended the state of war, the absence of a comprehensive treaty means diplomatic relations remain constrained by historical grievances (CNBC, 2026). Japan’s 2026 defense white paper explicitly highlights the Russian military buildup on these islands as a security concern, further complicating diplomatic negotiations.

What are the diplomatic consequences of the 2026 visit?

President Putin's 2026 visit to the island of Iturup, where he toured a fish-processing facility and met with regional leadership, was interpreted by Tokyo as a deliberate assertion of sovereignty. Japanese Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi issued a formal protest, asserting that such actions undermine the potential for future territorial compromise. For the international community, this incident serves as a reminder that frozen conflicts in the Pacific remain volatile and capable of disrupting regional stability with little notice.
