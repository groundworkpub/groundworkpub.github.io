What to do if your license plate has an offensive or unintended message

By Groundwork Editorial • 5 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

If you receive a license plate with an unintended offensive message, you can request a replacement through your DMV. Do not alter or cover the plate.

  • What is the process for replacing an offensive license plate?
  • How do states filter offensive license plate combinations?
  • What should you do if your vanity plate application was rejected?

A license plate is a state-issued identification tag for your vehicle that follows specific alphanumeric sequences assigned by your local Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV). While most plates are innocuous, random character assignments or vanity plate requests can occasionally result in unintended, offensive, or NSFW (not safe for work) combinations. If you receive a plate that you find embarrassing or inappropriate, you generally have the right to request a replacement, though the process and costs involved vary significantly by jurisdiction.

According to the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles, state agencies manage their own numbering systems and maintain lists of prohibited character combinations to minimize the risk of offensive plates reaching the public. Despite these safeguards, the sheer volume of plate combinations means that some unintended sequences may slip through the cracks.

Can you request a new license plate if you dislike your current one?

You can request a new license plate if your current one is offensive, damaged, or otherwise unacceptable to you. While state regulations differ, most DMVs have a formal process for replacing standard-issued plates. You are not typically required to keep a plate that causes you personal discomfort or social embarrassment.

To initiate the process, you must contact your state’s DMV or the equivalent motor vehicle agency. Because license plates are considered government property used for vehicle identification, toll collection, and law enforcement, you cannot simply remove or alter them yourself. Attempting to deface or modify an offensive plate is illegal and can lead to fines or citations, as law enforcement relies on these plates to track vehicle ownership and criminal activity (Legal Information Institute, 2023).

What is the process for replacing an offensive license plate?

The process for replacing an offensive license plate typically involves a formal application for a standard replacement. You should start by visiting your state’s official DMV website to determine if the request can be handled online or if an in-person visit is required.

1. Document the plate: Take a clear photo of your assigned plate to provide as evidence of the issue when you speak with DMV officials. 2. Contact the DMV: Call or email your local office to explain that the randomly assigned plate contains an offensive or unintended message. 3. Submit a replacement request: Fill out the necessary forms for a plate replacement. Be prepared to explain why the current plate is inappropriate. 4. Pay applicable fees: While some states may waive fees if the plate is deemed offensive, others will charge a standard replacement fee. Policies on fee waivers are at the discretion of the DMV representative. 5. Surrender the old plate: Once your replacement is approved, you will likely need to surrender the offensive plate to the DMV to ensure it is taken out of circulation.

How do states filter offensive license plate combinations?

States use a combination of automated software and human review to filter out offensive, profane, or potentially harmful license plate combinations before they are issued. This screening process applies to both standard-issued plates and custom vanity plates. According to the National Conference of State Legislatures, these filters are designed to exclude hate speech, sexually explicit language, references to criminal activity, and symbols that could mislead law enforcement officers.

Because language and slang are constantly evolving, DMV databases are updated regularly to include new banned terms. However, as noted by the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles, these systems are not infallible. Because random assignments are generated by computer algorithms, they may occasionally combine letters and numbers that create an unintended word or phrase in a specific regional dialect or slang context. When these combinations pass through the automated filter, they are only caught if a staff member happens to flag them during the manufacturing or distribution process.

What should you do if your vanity plate application was rejected?

If you applied for a custom vanity plate and it was rejected by the DMV, you generally have limited recourse. Most states reserve the right to deny any request they deem offensive or inappropriate under state law. Because vanity plates are a privilege rather than a right, the DMV’s decision is usually final.

If you believe your application was rejected in error—for example, if the plate is an acronym or a name that the system incorrectly identified as offensive—you may be able to appeal the decision. This usually requires submitting a written request explaining the intended meaning of the plate, along with any supporting documentation. However, if the DMV determines the plate could be interpreted as offensive by the general public, they are likely to uphold the rejection to avoid potential complaints or public controversy.

Why you should never alter your own license plate

You must never attempt to cover, modify, or deface your license plate, regardless of how offensive you find the message. License plates are essential tools for public safety. Law enforcement agencies use them for parking enforcement, identifying stolen vehicles, and tracking vehicles involved in accidents or criminal investigations.

If a police officer pulls you over and discovers that you have taped over, painted, or otherwise obscured your license plate, you could face significant legal consequences, including heavy fines, vehicle impoundment, or even criminal charges for attempting to evade identification. If you are uncomfortable with your plate, the only safe and legal path is to follow the formal replacement procedures established by your state’s motor vehicle agency.
