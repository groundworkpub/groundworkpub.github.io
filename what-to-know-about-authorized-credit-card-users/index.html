An authorized user is an individual who has been granted permission by a primary account holder to use a credit card linked to the primary holder’s account. While the authorized user receives a card bearing their own name, the legal responsibility for all charges remains solely with the primary cardholder.

At Groundwork, our analysis shows that adding an authorized user is a strategic financial tool that can accelerate rewards accumulation and assist with credit building, provided the relationship is managed with clear expectations and strict spending oversight.

What is the legal relationship of an authorized user? An authorized user is a person added to a primary account holder's credit card account who is granted the ability to make purchases, but who holds no legal liability for the debt incurred. While the primary cardholder is contractually obligated to pay the issuer for every transaction made by an authorized user, the user themselves is not responsible for repayment under the terms of the cardholder agreement (Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, 2023).

This distinction is vital: if an authorized user runs up a balance and refuses to pay, the primary cardholder is the one whose credit score and financial standing are at risk. The issuer does not pursue the authorized user for collections, as the primary holder signed the original credit agreement.

How does adding an authorized user affect your credit score? Adding an authorized user can significantly impact credit history, depending on the card issuer’s reporting policies. Many major issuers report the primary account's entire history—including payment history and credit utilization—to the credit bureaus under the authorized user’s name. This process is known as 'credit piggybacking.'

For an authorized user with little to no credit history, being added to an account with a long, positive track record and low utilization can provide an immediate boost to their credit score (Experian, 2024). Conversely, if the primary account holder misses payments or maintains high credit utilization, this negative information can also be reported to the credit bureaus, potentially damaging the authorized user's credit profile.

What are the primary benefits of adding an authorized user? Beyond credit building, authorized user status allows households to consolidate spending and maximize rewards. At Groundwork, our analysis indicates that the most common benefits include:

1. **Accelerated rewards:** By funneling household expenses through one account, you can meet minimum spending requirements for welcome bonuses much faster. 2. **Access to travel perks:** Many premium credit cards extend benefits to authorized users, such as airport lounge access, priority boarding, and hotel elite status. For instance, cards like the American Express Platinum Card® offer lounge access and hotel status to authorized users for a flat annual fee, which is often significantly lower than the cost of a primary cardholder’s annual fee. 3. **Simplified point transfers:** Some issuers, such as Chase, allow primary cardholders to transfer points directly to the loyalty program accounts of authorized users. This eliminates the need to pay fees for transferring points between disparate loyalty accounts.

What are the financial risks of this arrangement? Before adding someone to your account, you must assess the risk of unauthorized or excessive spending. Because the primary holder is legally liable, any spending by the authorized user becomes your debt immediately. If the user spends beyond your ability to pay, you face not only potential interest charges but also the risk of late payment marks on your own credit report (Federal Reserve, 2023).

To mitigate these risks, implement the following safeguards: - **Set spending limits:** If your issuer allows, set a specific transaction or monthly limit for the authorized user. - **Monitor activity:** Use mobile alerts to receive a notification for every transaction made on the account. - **Establish a repayment agreement:** Have a clear, written understanding of who is responsible for paying off charges made by the authorized user, and how those payments will be handled each month.

How to add or remove an authorized user Adding an authorized user is typically a streamlined process, while removing one requires a direct request to the issuer. Follow these steps to manage your account:

1. **Log into your issuer's mobile app or website:** Most issuers have a dedicated 'Manage Users' or 'Add Authorized User' section under account services. 2. **Provide the required information:** You will need the individual's full name, date of birth, and often their Social Security number to ensure the credit bureaus can accurately report the activity to their credit file. 3. **Receive and activate the card:** The card will be mailed to your address; ensure you supervise the delivery and activation process. 4. **Revoke access immediately if necessary:** If a relationship sours or the card is misused, call the number on the back of your card to remove the user. Once removed, request a new card for yourself to ensure any saved digital wallet information associated with the old card is invalidated.

At Groundwork, we emphasize that the convenience of an authorized user arrangement should never supersede the necessity of credit health. Prioritize your own financial security by only adding individuals who have demonstrated consistent financial responsibility.
