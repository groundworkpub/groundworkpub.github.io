Will there be a simpsons: hit & run remake?

By Groundwork Editorial • 4 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Is a Simpsons: Hit & Run remake coming? We analyze the legal hurdles, the D23 rumors, and the current reality of playing the 2003 cult classic today.

In This Guide

  • Why is there so much interest in a remake?
  • What are the legal barriers to a re-release?
  • How can you play the game today?
  • Should you expect an official announcement soon?
  • Actionable steps for fans

A potential revival of *The Simpsons: Hit & Run* is currently speculative, following comments made by creator Matt Groening at the D23 event suggesting the original title might return in some form. As of now, no official publisher, developer, or rights holder has confirmed a remake, remaster, or port of the 2003 cult classic for modern hardware.

According to industry tracking firm Statista, the market for video game remakes and remasters has grown significantly over the last decade, driven by digital storefronts and player nostalgia. While *The Simpsons: Hit & Run* remains a highly requested title, legal complexities regarding intellectual property rights between Disney, Fox, and various game publishers create significant barriers to a re-release.

Why is there so much interest in a remake?

*The Simpsons: Hit & Run* is a mission-based driving game that earned a cult following for its open-world parody of *Grand Theft Auto* set within the fictional town of Springfield. Released in 2003 by Radical Entertainment and published by Vivendi Universal Games, the title succeeded by blending authentic series writing with accessible, arcade-style gameplay. Its enduring popularity stems from a lack of modern alternatives; the game has not seen an official digital re-release since its original physical distribution, making it increasingly difficult to play on modern hardware.

For many fans, the game represents a "golden era" of licensed titles. Unlike many contemporary mobile-based Simpsons games, *Hit & Run* offered a self-contained, console-quality experience that rewarded exploration. The game’s map design, which allowed players to traverse iconic locations like Evergreen Terrace and the Kwik-E-Mart, remains a benchmark for licensed open-world design, according to retrospective analyses by IGN and GameSpot.

What are the legal barriers to a re-release?

Re-releasing a game from 2003 is rarely as simple as updating the code for modern consoles. The primary obstacle for *The Simpsons: Hit & Run* is a complex web of rights ownership. When the game was produced, the rights were held by different entities than those that currently control the *Simpsons* brand and the defunct publisher’s catalog. Following the acquisition of 21st Century Fox by The Walt Disney Company, the IP landscape became even more fragmented.

To bring the game to modern platforms, a publisher would need to clear music licenses, voice acting contracts, and the underlying software code rights. Many games from the early 2000s included licensed soundtracks or specific voice performances that were only cleared for the original distribution. Renegotiating these agreements is often prohibitively expensive, which is why many classic titles remain locked to their original platforms, as noted by industry legal experts at Kotaku.

How can you play the game today?

If you want to play *The Simpsons: Hit & Run* today, your options are limited to legacy hardware or community-driven workarounds. The game was originally released for the PlayStation 2, Nintendo GameCube, Xbox, and Windows. If you own these original consoles and a physical disc, this remains the only "official" way to experience the game.

For PC users, the community has kept the game alive through extensive modding. The "Donut Team" community, for instance, has developed mods that allow the game to run at higher resolutions, support widescreen monitors, and include custom content. While these mods improve the experience significantly, they require a legitimate original copy of the Windows version, which has become a collector’s item and often commands high prices on secondary markets like eBay.

Should you expect an official announcement soon?

Despite the excitement surrounding Matt Groening’s comments at D23, there is no empirical evidence to suggest a remake is currently in active development. In the gaming industry, offhand remarks by creators or showrunners often stem from a desire to be optimistic rather than inside knowledge of licensing negotiations.

If a remake were to happen, it would likely follow the path of other high-profile remakes like *Tony Hawk's Pro Skater 1 + 2*. This would require a significant financial investment from a publisher capable of navigating the Disney IP licensing structure. Until an official announcement appears via a verified press release from a major publisher (such as EA or Ubisoft, who have previously held Simpsons licenses), it is best to treat any rumors as speculation.

Actionable steps for fans

1. **Check your legacy hardware:** If you still own a PS2, GameCube, or original Xbox, verify that your disc is in working condition. 2. **Explore community forums:** Visit sites like the Donut Team to see how the PC modding community has updated the game's visuals and stability. 3. **Monitor official channels:** Follow official Disney Games and The Simpsons social media accounts for verified news, rather than relying on unconfirmed rumors from fan events. 4. **Avoid "remake" scams:** Be cautious of websites claiming to offer "remastered" downloads; these are often malware-heavy sites designed to exploit nostalgia. Only trust software from recognized, verified sources.
