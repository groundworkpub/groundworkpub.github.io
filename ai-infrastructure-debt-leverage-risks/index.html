The risks of AI infrastructure debt and leverage

👉 Read the Full Study on Groundwork: The risks of AI infrastructure debt and leverage

AI infrastructure debt is the accumulation of financial obligations—such as loans, bond issuances, and long-term lease commitments—used by corporations to fund the construction of data centers, power grids, and specialized hardware required to support artificial intelligence operations. As these investments scale to unprecedented levels, the complexity of these financing structures has created a hidden layer of market risk.

According to Goldman Sachs research, hyperscalers now hold combined lease commitments for data centers and equipment totaling $1.5 trillion, a staggering increase from $200 billion just five years ago.

Why is AI infrastructure debt difficult to track? AI infrastructure debt is difficult to track because much of it is hidden in "off-balance-sheet" structures, such as uncommenced lease commitments and joint ventures. When tech giants enter into these agreements, the full extent of their future payment obligations may not appear on standard financial statements until the leases actually commence.

Goldman Sachs analysts identified approximately $1 trillion in "uncommenced" lease commitments that currently exist outside of recognized debt figures (CNBC, 2026). This practice allows companies to secure massive infrastructure capacity while deferring the reporting of these liabilities, potentially understating the actual leverage held by the firm. As these contracts eventually move onto the balance sheet, they can create sudden, significant liquidity pressure that is not currently reflected in quarterly earnings reports.

How are investors amplifying their risk through leverage? Investors are amplifying their risk by using prime brokerage borrowing and derivative instruments to increase their exposure to AI-related stocks. By using borrowed capital to bet on the growth of the AI sector, hedge funds and institutional investors can magnify returns during market upswings, but they also face accelerated losses when market sentiment shifts.

The collapse of the AI-focused hedge fund Situational Awareness serves as a primary case study for this risk. When leveraged equity bets fail, the resulting forced liquidations can create a cascading effect across the broader market. Because this leverage is often buried within complex derivative structures, it remains difficult for regulators and observers to determine exactly how much capital is at risk or how quickly a "deleveraging" event could occur if AI growth expectations fail to meet current projections.

What are the risks of treating AI as an 'investable asset'? Treating AI infrastructure as an investable asset involves shifting the financing of data centers from traditional corporate debt to private credit and asset-based financing models. This transition is being led by partnerships between tech leaders like Nvidia and major private equity firms including Apollo, Blackstone, and KKR.

While this model provides companies with the capital needed to expand rapidly, it ties the stability of major financial institutions to the performance of specific hardware and data center capacity. If the revenue generated by AI applications does not justify the massive capital expenditure (capex) cycle, these asset-based loans could face significant impairment. As Lotfi Karoui of PIMCO notes, this is on track to be the largest investment cycle in history, adjusted for inflation; the scale of this bet leaves little room for error if the anticipated "AI productivity boom" arrives slower than expected.

How should you manage your exposure to AI infrastructure risks? To manage your exposure to AI infrastructure risks, you must look beyond top-line revenue growth and analyze the debt-to-equity ratios and lease obligations of companies heavily invested in AI. If a company relies on opaque financing structures to fund its growth, it is inherently more vulnerable to interest rate spikes and liquidity crunches.

1. Review the "Commitments and Contingencies" section of corporate 10-K filings to identify off-balance-sheet lease obligations. 2. Monitor the interest coverage ratio of companies with high capital expenditure cycles to ensure they can service their debt even if AI revenue growth slows. 3. Diversify away from pure-play AI infrastructure bets to mitigate the risk of a sector-wide liquidity event. 4. Prioritize firms with high cash reserves, as they are less reliant on the volatile debt markets currently fueling the infrastructure boom.
