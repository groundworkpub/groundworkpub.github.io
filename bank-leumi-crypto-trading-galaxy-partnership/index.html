What the bank leumi and galaxy partnership means for crypto investors

By Groundwork Editorial • 4 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Bank Leumi is partnering with Galaxy Digital to offer crypto trading by 2027. Learn how this shift affects your ability to trade digital assets via banks.

In This Guide

  • How will the bank leumi crypto service function?
  • Why are banks moving toward crypto integration?
  • What are the risks of trading crypto through a bank?
  • How to prepare for banking-led crypto adoption

A crypto-trading partnership is a collaborative agreement between a traditional financial institution and a specialized digital asset provider to offer cryptocurrency services directly through a bank’s existing interface. Bank Leumi, Israel’s largest financial institution, recently announced a partnership with Galaxy Digital to allow customers to buy, hold, and sell bitcoin, ether, and solana directly within the Leumi Trade mobile application starting in early 2027.

This development signifies a shift in how legacy banking institutions approach digital assets. By integrating these services, Bank Leumi is positioning itself as the first major Israeli bank to offer direct access to crypto, moving beyond the traditional "buy-and-hold" model typically seen in standalone exchange environments. According to CoinDesk, the partnership utilizes Galaxy’s "GalaxyOne" platform, which provides the necessary infrastructure for institutional-grade trading and custodial services (CoinDesk, 2026).

How will the bank leumi crypto service function?

The service will operate as an embedded feature within the existing Leumi Trade application, allowing users to manage digital assets alongside traditional securities. By utilizing Galaxy Digital’s infrastructure, the bank aims to streamline the user experience, eliminating the need for customers to transfer funds to third-party exchanges. This integration is designed to provide a more familiar, regulated environment for investors who may be wary of the security risks associated with independent crypto platforms.

For most customers, this means the process will likely follow these steps: 1. Access your existing Leumi Trade mobile application. 2. Navigate to the newly integrated digital assets section. 3. Complete any required identity verification or risk disclosures specific to crypto trading. 4. Execute trades for supported assets like bitcoin, ether, or solana. 5. View your digital asset holdings alongside your traditional investment portfolio in one consolidated dashboard.

By keeping these assets within the bank's ecosystem, Bank Leumi is betting that clients prefer the oversight and security infrastructure of a regulated bank over the relative autonomy of a standalone crypto exchange. The custody component, provided by Galaxy’s infrastructure (formerly known as GK8), ensures that the underlying assets are held in a manner consistent with institutional security standards.

Why are banks moving toward crypto integration?

Banks are increasingly integrating digital assets because they recognize that cryptocurrency has become a significant asset class within the global financial system. According to Maya Ravia, Bank Leumi’s head of strategy, digital assets are becoming an integral component of modern finance. By offering these services, banks can retain assets that might otherwise flow out to external platforms, while also collecting fees associated with trading and custody services.

Furthermore, this move allows financial institutions to capture a younger, tech-savvy demographic that expects their bank to provide a "one-stop-shop" for all investment types. As the financial landscape shifts toward open and programmable infrastructure, early movers like Bank Leumi aim to define the standards for how these assets are handled, reported, and taxed within a regulated framework.

What are the risks of trading crypto through a bank?

While banking integration offers convenience, it does not eliminate the inherent volatility of the cryptocurrency market. Investors should be aware that trading digital assets through a bank does not protect them against market-wide price drops. Additionally, trading fees and service charges are often higher at traditional financial institutions compared to specialized, high-volume crypto exchanges.

Before you engage with any bank-offered crypto service, consider the following: - **Fee structures:** Banks may charge premiums for the convenience of an integrated interface. - **Asset selection:** Banks typically offer a limited selection of "blue-chip" cryptocurrencies, meaning you may not have access to smaller or emerging tokens. - **Control and custody:** Ensure you understand whether you have full custody of your assets or if they are held in a custodial account by the bank. - **Regulatory status:** Confirm how the bank handles tax reporting and compliance, as this can differ significantly from the requirements of standalone exchanges.

How to prepare for banking-led crypto adoption

If you are an investor looking to take advantage of institutional crypto services, start by evaluating your current investment goals. If you value security and simplicity over lower fees and a wider variety of assets, waiting for a trusted banking partner to offer these services is a sound strategy.

1. Review your current portfolio allocation to determine what percentage should be dedicated to volatile assets like cryptocurrency. 2. Monitor your bank’s announcements for specific fee structures and eligibility requirements, as these were not disclosed in the initial Bank Leumi partnership announcement. 3. Ensure your identity verification with your bank is up to date, as institutional platforms will require strict KYC (Know Your Customer) compliance. 4. Research the security protocols of the bank’s chosen infrastructure partner—in this case, Galaxy Digital—to understand how they protect your assets from cyber threats.

By taking a measured approach, you can benefit from the convenience of banking-integrated crypto trading without sacrificing the diligence required for successful long-term investing. While the landscape is evolving, the move toward regulated, institutional-grade access is a sign that the digital asset market is maturing into a more permanent fixture of the broader financial ecosystem.
