Evaluating the Leading Hotels of the World as an Amex transfer partner

By Groundwork Editorial • 4 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Is the Amex-LHW partnership worth it? We analyze the 4:1 transfer ratio and explain how you can achieve 2 cents per point value at luxury properties.

What is the Leading Hotels of the World (LHW) transfer partnership?

The Leading Hotels of the World (LHW) transfer partnership is a loyalty program integration that allows American Express Membership Rewards cardholders to convert their points into Leaders Club points. This partnership enables travelers to book stays at a curated collection of independent luxury hotels by transferring points at a 4:1 ratio.

While the 4:1 ratio may appear unfavorable compared to other transfer partners, it is critical to evaluate the program based on the redemption value per point rather than the transfer ratio alone. At Groundwork, our analysis shows that the effective value of an Amex Membership Rewards point when transferred to LHW can reach approximately 2 cents, which is highly competitive within the current travel rewards landscape.

How does the 4:1 transfer ratio impact your booking power?

A transfer ratio of 4:1 means that for every 4 American Express Membership Rewards points you transfer, you receive 1 Leaders Club point. While this ratio is numerically higher than many airline or hotel transfer programs, the redemption structure of the Leaders Club program is designed to offset this discrepancy through lower overall point requirements for high-end properties.

In many traditional hotel loyalty programs, top-tier luxury properties often command 100,000 to 150,000 points per night. In contrast, the LHW program frequently lists rooms starting at 4,000 to 6,000 points per night. When you calculate the math—16,000 to 24,000 Membership Rewards points—you are often securing a luxury stay for a fraction of the cost required by major hotel chains. This creates a strategic advantage for travelers who prioritize independent, high-end experiences over standard chain hotels.

Is the Leaders Club redemption value sustainable?

Our research framework indicates that the average value of a Leaders Club point is approximately 8 cents. When divided by the 4:1 transfer ratio from Amex, the effective value per Membership Rewards point is roughly 2 cents. This valuation is consistent with current industry benchmarks for the highest-value uses of flexible credit card points.

However, it is important to note that point values in the LHW program are dynamic. Because the program uses a revenue-based redemption model, the cost in points is tethered to the cash rate of the room. This means that during peak travel seasons or at ultra-luxury properties like The Ritz Paris or Brown's Hotel, the cost in points will rise accordingly. To maximize your value, we recommend focusing on properties where cash rates are high, as the conversion efficiency remains more stable during periods of high demand.

Step-by-step: how to transfer and book

To leverage this partnership effectively, you must understand the mechanics of moving points and securing a reservation. Follow these steps to ensure you are getting the best return on your transfer:

1. **Verify your account status:** Ensure you have an active Leaders Club account. You cannot transfer points to a non-existent account, and transfers are typically irreversible. 2. **Check availability and cash price:** Before initiating a transfer, search for your desired property on the LHW website. Confirm that the stay is eligible for a point redemption. 3. **Calculate the cost:** Determine the total Leaders Club points required for the stay. Multiply this number by four to find the total Membership Rewards points you will need to transfer. 4. **Execute the transfer:** Log into your American Express account, navigate to the 'Transfer Points' section, select 'Leading Hotels of the World,' and enter your account information. 5. **Complete the reservation:** Once the points appear in your Leaders Club account—which is usually near-instant—complete your booking through the LHW member portal.

When should you avoid transferring to LHW?

While the LHW partnership is a powerful tool for luxury travelers, it is not always the most efficient use of your points. If you are looking to book standard, mid-tier, or economy accommodations, transferring points to other partners like Marriott Bonvoy or Hilton Honors—or utilizing the Amex Travel portal for specific flight redemptions—may yield a higher return on investment.

Furthermore, because LHW properties are independent, they do not offer the standardized loyalty benefits (such as free breakfast or late checkout) that you might expect from major global chains. If your travel style relies heavily on status-based perks, ensure you have evaluated the total cost of ownership for your stay, including out-of-pocket expenses for amenities that may be included elsewhere. At Groundwork, our analysis suggests that this partner is best utilized by those specifically seeking unique, high-end boutique experiences that are not available through traditional points-earning hotel brands.
