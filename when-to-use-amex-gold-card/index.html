When to use your American Express Gold Card

The American Express Gold Card is a rewards-based credit card that incentivizes food-related spending by offering elevated point earnings at U.S. supermarkets and restaurants worldwide. The card is designed for individuals whose monthly budget is heavily weighted toward grocery shopping and dining out, providing a balance between a mid-tier annual fee and high-value travel rewards.

According to data from The Points Guy, the Amex Gold offers a 4x points return on up to $25,000 in U.S. supermarket spending and $50,000 in restaurant spending annually, translating to an estimated 8% return based on current point valuations (The Points Guy, 2026). While these rates are competitive, maximizing the card requires understanding exactly where it outperforms general-purpose cards and where it falls short.

When to use your Amex Gold

You should prioritize the Amex Gold whenever you are making purchases that fall into its primary bonus categories. These categories are structured to capture the bulk of a household’s food-related expenses.

1. **Dining at restaurants:** You earn 4 points per dollar on dining worldwide. This category is broad and covers everything from fine dining to fast-casual establishments. 2. **U.S. supermarket purchases:** You earn 4 points per dollar on the first $25,000 spent at U.S. supermarkets each calendar year. This is particularly effective for families with significant grocery bills. 3. **Flights booked directly or via Amex Travel:** The card earns 3 points per dollar on airfare, making it a viable option for occasional travelers who do not want a dedicated airline or ultra-premium travel card. 4. **Prepaid hotel stays:** Booking through the Amex Travel portal earns 5 points per dollar, which is one of the highest earning rates for hotel bookings available on a non-hotel-specific card.

When to use a different card

Despite its strengths in the food category, the Amex Gold is not an optimal "catch-all" card. If you use it for every purchase, you are likely leaving value on the table.

General spending For all purchases outside of the bonus categories, the Amex Gold earns only 1 point per dollar. If you have a flat-rate cash-back card that earns 2% on all purchases, you are effectively choosing a lower return by using your Gold card for non-category items like hardware store runs, utility bills, or retail shopping. Research suggests that consumers who diversify their wallet with a flat-rate card for non-bonus spending see higher overall annual rewards (Bankrate, 2025).

High-end travel benefits If you are a frequent traveler who relies on airport lounge access, complimentary hotel status, or high-tier travel protections, the Amex Gold may be insufficient. Cards with higher annual fees, such as the American Express Platinum Card, offer comprehensive lounge access and airline incidentals that the Gold card lacks. Relying on the Gold card for travel booking when you have a premium card with better trip delay or cancellation insurance is a common oversight.

Strategic credit card management

To derive the most value from the Amex Gold, you must be disciplined about the card’s statement credits. The card currently carries a $325 annual fee (American Express, 2026). It provides various statement credits for dining and travel partners. If you do not naturally spend money with these specific partners, the "value" of these credits is theoretical rather than actual.

Before deciding to keep the card, conduct a simple audit: 1. List your total annual spend in the 4x categories (supermarkets and restaurants). 2. Calculate the points earned (4x your spend). 3. Subtract the $325 annual fee from the cash value of those points. 4. Determine if the remaining value is higher than what you would earn with a no-annual-fee card.

If the math does not result in a net gain, it may be time to switch to a card with a lower cost of ownership or a different reward structure that better matches your spending habits.
