How institutional wealth managers are accessing crypto liquidity in Asia

By Marcus Vance • 4 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Institutional crypto adoption is rising as family offices use liquidity providers to access digital assets. Learn how professional firms bridge this gap.

Outline

  • Why are institutional investors turning to crypto in Asia?
  • How do liquidity providers serve traditional wealth managers?
  • What role do veteran finance leaders play in crypto firms?
  • How should family offices approach digital asset allocation?

Institutional crypto adoption is the process by which traditional financial firms, family offices, and asset managers incorporate digital assets into their portfolios using professional-grade liquidity providers. As interest from wealthy investors grows, firms like B2C2 are bridging the gap between traditional finance and decentralized markets by hiring veteran wealth management executives to build trust and infrastructure.

Recent industry data suggests that approximately one-third of family offices globally now hold positions in cryptocurrencies, according to research from Goldman Sachs. This shift marks a transition from retail-led speculation to a more structured approach where institutional players prioritize security, execution quality, and regulatory compliance.

Why are institutional investors turning to crypto in Asia?

Institutional investors are entering the crypto market to diversify portfolios, hedge against traditional market volatility, and capture growth in the digital asset sector. In the Asia-Pacific region, this trend is driven by a massive accumulation of private wealth and a strategic push by financial hubs like Singapore and Hong Kong to regulate digital asset services. As these jurisdictions establish clear legal frameworks, large-scale asset managers are finding it safer to deploy capital into crypto-native liquidity providers.

According to data from Chainalysis, the Asia-Pacific region saw transaction volumes surge by 69% to $2.36 trillion in the year leading up to June 2025, making it the fastest-growing region for on-chain activity globally. This volume is not driven solely by retail traders; it reflects a maturing market where institutional "smart money" is seeking reliable partners to manage high-frequency execution and large-block trades without causing significant price slippage.

How do liquidity providers serve traditional wealth managers?

Liquidity providers act as the "middlemen" in the crypto ecosystem, ensuring that institutional investors can enter or exit large positions at predictable prices. Unlike retail exchanges, which may struggle with deep liquidity for multi-million dollar trades, institutional liquidity providers (LPs) use proprietary trading technology to offer 24/7 access to spot, derivatives, and over-the-counter (OTC) markets. These firms provide the essential infrastructure that allows a traditional family office to treat digital assets with the same professional rigor as they would equities or bonds.

For an institutional client, the process typically involves: 1. Establishing a relationship with a regulated liquidity provider that offers strict KYC (Know Your Customer) and AML (Anti-Money Laundering) compliance. 2. Integrating via API or professional trading platforms to ensure seamless execution. 3. Utilizing OTC desks to execute large orders off the public order book, which prevents immediate price impact and maintains anonymity. 4. Implementing structured products that allow for risk management, such as options or yield-generating strategies, which are increasingly demanded by sophisticated wealth managers.

What role do veteran finance leaders play in crypto firms?

Crypto firms are increasingly hiring leaders from traditional finance (TradFi) to overcome the "trust gap" that exists between decentralized technology and institutional legacy systems. By appointing figures like former Schroders Wealth Management Asia chairman Jason Lai, crypto liquidity providers gain the credibility needed to navigate the conservative culture of private wealth management. These leaders bring decades of experience in asset allocation, client relationship management, and regulatory navigation, which are vital for firms looking to scale within the strict confines of Asian financial regulations.

When a firm brings on a veteran advisor, they are essentially signaling to the market that their operations meet the standards expected by pension funds, endowments, and high-net-worth individuals. This professionalization is a necessary step for the industry as it moves toward a state where digital assets are considered a standard component of a diversified institutional portfolio.

How should family offices approach digital asset allocation?

If you are managing or advising a family office, the entry into digital assets requires a methodical approach that prioritizes risk management over speculative gains. The volatility inherent in crypto markets means that exposure should be size-limited and integrated into a broader asset allocation strategy.

1. Conduct due diligence on liquidity partners: Ensure the firm is backed by reputable parent companies and maintains institutional-grade security protocols. 2. Define your mandate: Determine whether you are looking for simple price exposure (spot) or complex yield strategies (structured products). 3. Focus on regulatory compliance: Prioritize jurisdictions like Singapore or Hong Kong where there is a clear "rulebook" for digital asset custodians and brokers. 4. Start with small, liquid positions: Use OTC desks to execute initial trades to avoid the volatility of public retail exchanges.

As the market continues to mature, the distinction between "crypto" and "finance" will likely continue to blur. The successful integration of digital assets into the portfolios of the world’s wealthiest families is not just a trend; it is a fundamental shift in how capital is managed in the 21st century. By leveraging the expertise of traditional finance veterans and the speed of crypto-native liquidity, institutional investors are positioning themselves to participate in the growth of digital infrastructure while maintaining the oversight and professional standards required by their stakeholders.
