How comedian Chloe Radcliffe is reframing the conversation around cheating

By Alex Rivera • 3 min read • Updated 2026-08-21

Key Takeaway

Comedian Chloe Radcliffe is reframing the conversation on infidelity by moving beyond moral judgment to explore the psychological patterns behind cheating.

Outline

  • Why does society struggle to discuss infidelity?
  • How comedy serves as a tool for behavioral inquiry
  • Can changing the narrative reduce the frequency of cheating?
  • How to approach your own relationship patterns

Infidelity is a complex behavioral pattern often reduced to moral failure in public discourse. Comedian and writer Chloe Radcliffe is challenging this binary view through her solo show, *Cheat*, which uses personal narrative to explore the psychological underpinnings of infidelity. By moving away from the traditional moral condemnation of cheaters, Radcliffe argues that current societal approaches to the topic may actually be counterproductive to understanding or reducing the behavior.

Why does society struggle to discuss infidelity?

Societal discourse surrounding cheating is often rooted in rigid moral judgment rather than psychological analysis. According to Radcliffe, the current cultural framework for discussing infidelity lacks nuance, often placing cheaters in a category that receives less empathy than even violent offenders. This social stigma creates a barrier to honest reflection. When an action is treated purely as a character flaw, the underlying emotional, relational, or psychological drivers are rarely examined. Research suggests that infidelity is rarely a singular event but rather a byproduct of unmet needs, communication failures, or individual attachment styles (Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 2021). By refusing to accept a one-dimensional villain label, Radcliffe’s work encourages an examination of the systemic and individual factors that contribute to relationship breaches.

How comedy serves as a tool for behavioral inquiry

Comedy is a powerful medium for deconstructing taboo topics because it creates a safe distance for the audience to engage with uncomfortable truths. Radcliffe uses her platform to strip away the shame typically associated with cheating, allowing her audience to view the behavior through a lens of curiosity rather than judgment. This approach mirrors the psychological concept of self-distancing, where individuals can gain a more objective perspective on their own behaviors by viewing them through a narrative lens (Psychological Science, 2014). By performing her experiences on stage, Radcliffe transforms personal history into a shared inquiry, helping the audience identify patterns in their own lives that they might otherwise ignore.

Can changing the narrative reduce the frequency of cheating?

Radcliffe posits that the current way we talk about cheating does not actually help people cheat less. If the goal of social discourse is to foster healthier relationships, then shame-based narratives are objectively ineffective. Shame is known to inhibit positive behavioral change, often leading individuals to hide or repeat destructive patterns (Journal of Clinical Psychology, 2018). Instead, open, non-judgmental discussions about the realities of long-term commitment and the temptations that arise within them can lead to more resilient relationships. By normalizing the conversation, she suggests that individuals can address their impulses or relationship dissatisfaction before they manifest as infidelity.

How to approach your own relationship patterns

If you find yourself reflecting on your own relationship history or current dynamics, consider the following steps to move toward healthier communication:

1. Identify your triggers: Recognize the specific stressors or emotional voids that lead to a desire for external validation. 2. Practice radical honesty: Initiate conversations about needs and boundaries before they become points of resentment. 3. Seek professional support: Use therapy to explore attachment patterns rather than relying on internal moralizing. 4. De-stigmatize the conversation: Discuss relationship challenges with partners or peers without the pressure of binary labels like "good" or "bad."

By shifting the focus from blame to understanding, you can better navigate the complexities of long-term partnership. Radcliffe’s work serves as a reminder that understanding the "why" behind our actions is the first step toward building the "what" of a healthier future.
