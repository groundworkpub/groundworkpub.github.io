A product launch represents the official introduction of new hardware and software to the consumer market, serving as the primary window into a company's strategic direction for the coming year. In the case of the Made by Google 2026 event, the company preemptively disclosed the specifications and features of its upcoming hardware, including the Pixel 11 series, the Pixel 11 Pro Fold, and the Pixel Watch 5. This article breaks down the technical upgrades and changes to the product lineup based on the company’s official disclosures.

The Pixel 11 series: design and hardware refinements

The Pixel 11 series consists of three primary models: the Pixel 11, Pixel 11 Pro, and Pixel 11 Pro XL. According to Google’s disclosures, the design language remains largely consistent with the previous generation, maintaining the recognizable oval-shaped camera housing on the rear and a hole-punch display on the front (CNET, 2026).

However, there are notable physical refinements. Google has reduced the thickness of the camera bar by 40%. When paired with official cases, this results in a nearly flush rear profile. For the Pro models, Google has increased peak display brightness to 3,600 nits and implemented a new anti-scratch coating. The company claims this coating provides double the scratch resistance of the Pixel 10 Pro, marking a significant step forward in device durability (Google, 2026).

Internal performance: the Tensor G6 chip

At the core of the new lineup is the Tensor G6 processor. Performance benchmarks provided by Google indicate a shift in how the device handles both standard tasks and on-device machine learning. The G6 chip offers a 25% increase in web browsing speed and a 15% improvement in application launch times compared to the Tensor G5.

Perhaps more significantly, the Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) has been optimized for on-device AI. Google reports that the G6 can process AI functions 3.5 times faster than its predecessor while maintaining 3.5 times greater energy efficiency (Google, 2026). This optimization is critical for users who rely on local processing for photography, voice-to-text, and real-time translation features, as it reduces the reliance on cloud-based servers and extends battery life during heavy processing tasks.

Changes to storage tiers and pricing

A pivotal change for the 2026 lineup is the elimination of the 128GB base storage tier. All models in the Pixel 11 series will now start at 256GB. While this provides more space for high-resolution media and applications, it effectively raises the starting price of the entry-level device to $899. This move aligns with broader industry trends where manufacturers are phasing out lower-capacity storage options to accommodate the increasing file sizes of AI-enhanced media and high-definition software (Lifehacker, 2026).

New functional features: HiLight and accessory integration

Google has introduced a feature called "HiLight," an LED integration within the camera bar. This light functions as a visual notification system, allowing users to assign specific colors to individual contacts or applications. This enables users to identify incoming alerts while the phone is placed face-down, providing a discreet way to manage notifications without active screen engagement.

Beyond the phones, Google has signaled expansion into the tracking market with the introduction of the Pixel Tag. This device serves as a direct competitor to existing Bluetooth trackers like the Apple AirTag. Coupled with the Pixel Watch 5 and the Pixel 11 Pro Fold, these additions suggest a push toward a more cohesive ecosystem where hardware peripherals are tightly integrated with the core smartphone experience. Users should evaluate whether these ecosystem features provide enough value to justify the higher "entry price" created by the removal of the 128GB storage tier.
