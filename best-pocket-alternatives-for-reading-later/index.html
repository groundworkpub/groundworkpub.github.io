A read-it-later app is a digital tool designed to store web articles, videos, and newsletters in a simplified, distraction-free interface for offline or later consumption. With the shutdown of Mozilla’s Pocket in 2025, many users are seeking new platforms to manage their saved content and maintain a curated reading list.

Following the industry shift, market data indicates that users are prioritizing apps with cross-platform synchronization, offline reading capabilities, and text-to-speech functionality. While Pocket was a dominant player, the current ecosystem offers more specialized tools that cater to different workflows, ranging from simple bookmark management to AI-enhanced research assistants.

How to choose the right read-it-later app Choosing the right app depends on whether you prioritize simple organization or advanced features like audio narration and AI insights. Evaluate your needs based on the platform's primary function: bookmark management, deep-reading environments, or multimedia consumption. Most modern alternatives offer free tiers, allowing you to test the interface before committing to a subscription.

Matter: best for audio and AI features Matter is a specialized reading app that focuses on multimedia integration and AI-assisted comprehension. The app includes a sophisticated text-to-speech engine that converts articles into high-quality audio, making it a strong choice for commuters or those who prefer listening to content. It also features an AI co-reader tool that allows you to ask questions about the articles you are consuming, effectively turning static text into an interactive experience.

While the base version of Matter is free, the premium tier—priced at approximately $79.99 per year—unlocks advanced features such as improved podcast transcriptions, YouTube video summaries, and deeper integration with productivity tools like Notion, Obsidian, and Kindle. If your goal is to synthesize information across multiple formats, Matter is currently the market leader for power users.

Instapaper: best for traditional reading and stability Instapaper is a legacy read-it-later service that prioritizes a clean, distraction-free reading environment. Founded in 2008, it remains one of the most reliable options for users who want to strip away web clutter and focus purely on text. It functions effectively on both iOS and Android, offering a seamless experience for saving long-form journalism.

For most users, the free version of Instapaper provides sufficient functionality, including unlimited article saving and offline access. However, the premium subscription, which costs $59.99 per year, adds advanced features such as full-text search, permanent archival of saved content, and the ability to send articles directly to a Kindle e-reader. For users who value long-term archiving and a classic "newspaper" feel, Instapaper is the most direct successor to the Pocket experience.

Raindrop.io: best for bookmark management and organization Raindrop.io is primarily a bookmark manager that excels at organizing diverse types of digital content, including articles, PDFs, images, and videos. Unlike dedicated reading apps that strip away original formatting, Raindrop.io acts as a visual library for your digital life. Its browser extensions and mobile apps provide a robust way to categorize items using tags, folders, and nested collections.

The free version allows for unlimited bookmarks and basic organization. The Pro version, priced at $33 per year, introduces powerful tools such as full-text search, duplicate link detection, and broken link identification. This makes it the ideal choice if your primary struggle is not just "reading later" but maintaining an organized, searchable database of every link you have ever saved.

How to migrate your data To move your data from a legacy platform to a new app, follow these steps:

1. Use the export tool provided by your current service to download your archive in a standard format like HTML or CSV. 2. Verify the file size and ensure all metadata, such as tags and dates, are included in the export. 3. Locate the "Import" function in your new app’s settings menu. 4. Upload your file and wait for the system to process your saved items. 5. Test a few links to ensure that the new app correctly parses the content and maintains your desired formatting.

Note that some platforms may have specific import limitations or file size caps, so verify these requirements before initiating a large transfer.
