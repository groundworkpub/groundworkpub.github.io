How to choose a robot lawnmower for your yard

A robot lawnmower is an autonomous machine designed to maintain grass height by cutting small amounts of turf frequently, eliminating the need for weekly manual mowing. Modern models have moved beyond basic random-pattern navigation to utilize satellite-based positioning, offering precision that creates a healthier, thicker lawn through consistent, daily maintenance.

Recent data indicates that the shift from wire-based boundary systems to satellite-guided navigation has reduced setup time by approximately 70% and significantly decreased the frequency of machines getting stuck or navigating outside designated zones (Source: The Verge, 2024).

How does modern navigation tech work?

Modern robot lawnmowers primarily use Real-Time Kinematic (RTK) positioning to navigate your yard with centimeter-level accuracy. RTK relies on satellite signals combined with a stationary reference station to determine the mower's exact location, replacing the labor-intensive boundary wires that required digging into your soil.

To ensure consistent performance, many manufacturers now incorporate secondary navigation systems such as vSLAM (visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) or Lidar. These systems act as a fallback when GPS signals are obscured by heavy tree cover, rooflines, or dense foliage. If you have a yard with significant obstacles, prioritize models that offer multiple sensor inputs to maintain navigation when satellite connectivity drops.

What are the benefits of daily autonomous mowing?

Autonomous mowers provide a distinct agronomic advantage by trimming only a few millimeters of grass each day. This process, known as mulching, returns vital nutrients back into the soil, which leads to a visibly lusher and more resilient lawn over a single growing season.

Beyond aesthetics, these machines operate at decibel levels significantly lower than traditional gas-powered mowers. This allows for quiet, "set-and-forget" operation that does not disturb pets, local wildlife, or neighbors. Because the machines work on a continuous schedule, they prevent the weed proliferation often associated with allowing grass to grow long between traditional weekly cuts.

What are the limitations of current technology?

Despite improvements in navigation, robot mowers are not fully autonomous devices that require zero oversight. They are heavy machines equipped with high-speed blades, meaning they require regular inspection to ensure they have not become stuck, damaged by debris, or wandered into restricted areas.

Network RTK (NetRTK) is an alternative to physical antennas that leverages existing regional reference stations via cloud connectivity. While this simplifies installation, coverage is not universal. You must verify that your specific property falls within a supported NetRTK network area before relying on this technology for navigation. Always perform a test run of the device while you are present to ensure it understands your yard’s unique layout.

How to prepare your yard for a robot mower

1. Map your yard boundaries: Use the companion app to guide the mower along your property edges to create a digital map. This replaces the old method of burying perimeter wire. 2. Clear surface debris: Remove large rocks, thick branches, and children’s toys that could obstruct the mower or damage the blades. 3. Check your Wi-Fi and cellular signal: Ensure the areas where you plan to use the mower have adequate connectivity for the machine to receive updates and maintain cloud-based navigation data. 4. Install the reference station: If your model requires a physical RTK antenna, place it in a location with a clear, unobstructed view of the sky to maximize signal strength. 5. Conduct a supervised test run: Observe the mower during its first few complete cycles to identify "blind spots" or areas where it consistently struggles with navigation, then adjust the map accordingly.
